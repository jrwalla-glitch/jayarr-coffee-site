Stumptown and Blue Bottle are two of the best-known names from the generation of American roasters that came to be called the third wave. This comparison uses the companies' own pages, checked October 18, 2026, for what they sell now, and dated releases and filings for who owns them. For the movement itself, see our [history of third wave coffee](/blog/third-wave-coffee-history-movement/).

## Where each one came from

**Stumptown** was founded in Portland, Oregon, in 1999, the company says. Its first café opened on Southeast Division Street in a former beauty parlor called Hair Bender, which gave Stumptown's signature espresso blend its name. Stumptown dates its Direct Trade buying to 2003, beginning with the Aguirre family of Finca El Injerto in Guatemala, a partnership it says continues today.

Stumptown's founder, Duane Sorenson, is one of the three buyers the writer Michaele Weissman featured in her 2008 book *God in a Cup*, alongside Geoff Watts of Intelligentsia and Peter Giuliano of Counter Culture, as the historian Mark Pendergrast recounts in *Uncommon Grounds*. Weissman called them "third wave" coffee people. The term itself is credited to Trish Rothgeb, who used it in an essay about Norway's baristas printed in the Roasters Guild's newsletter in spring 2003, and later founded Wrecking Ball Coffee Roasters; our third wave page tells that story.

**Blue Bottle** began in Oakland, California, with its founder, James Freeman, and, in the company's words, "a single cart selling roasted coffee beans at the Old Oakland Farmers Market." Blue Bottle's own history doesn't give a year. Nestlé, announcing its stake in September 2017, said Blue Bottle had "achieved iconic status amongst discerning coffee drinkers" over the previous 15 years. Its Hayes Valley espresso is named, Blue Bottle says, after the San Francisco neighborhood of its first brick-and-mortar location. Blue Bottle wasn't one of the three roasters in Weissman's book.

An earlier version of this page called Stumptown and Blue Bottle "two of the four roasters who built American third-wave coffee" and said both were named in *God in a Cup*; Pendergrast's account names three buyers, and Blue Bottle isn't one of them. It also dated details of Blue Bottle's early years (the day of the first market, a potting shed, the first kiosk) that we couldn't find in a primary source, so they're gone.

## The coffees side by side

Both companies sell several blends and rotating single origins. These are the flagships, as each company describes them:

| As listed when checked October 18, 2026 | Stumptown | Blue Bottle |
|---|---|---|
| Flagship blend | Hair Bender | Three Africas |
| Its notes | Citrus, dark chocolate, raisin | Golden raisin, winey blueberry, lemon zest |
| What's in it | Latin America, Indonesia and East Africa | Two Ethiopians, washed and natural, and a Rwandan |
| Roast, as labeled | Not stated in its description | Medium |
| Price, 12 oz | $19 | $23 |
| Espresso blend | Hair Bender | Hayes Valley Espresso, dark |
| Organic picks | Holler Mountain, Founder's Blend | Three Africas, Bella Donovan, Hayes Valley |

At those prices, Stumptown's flagship works out to about $1.58 an ounce and Blue Bottle's to about $1.92 (calculated, as listed when checked October 18, 2026), before shipping.

**Stumptown's blends.** Hair Bender, Stumptown says, is "the sweet, balanced coffee that started it all," its go-to espresso in every Stumptown café and also a cup for pour-over, drip or French press. Holler Mountain, its organic blend, is "medium roasted," with citrus and berry jam up front and caramel to round them out, listed at $20 for 12 oz when checked October 18, 2026. Founder's Blend, also organic, is the chocolate-forward one, with vanilla, cocoa and honey, at $20 for 12 oz. Stumptown no longer lists the House Blend an earlier version of this page described.

**Blue Bottle's blends.** Three Africas, Blue Bottle says, is "our blend for the single origin lovers," roasted "with a light touch," yet "dark enough for a rounded, full-bodied cup." Bella Donovan, which it labels a dark roast, is its take on the classic Moka-Java profile, with raspberry, chocolate and molasses, at $23 for 12 oz. Hayes Valley Espresso, also labeled dark, is the base of Blue Bottle's café drinks, with notes of baking chocolate, orange zest and brown sugar, listed at $22 for 12 oz when checked October 18, 2026.

An earlier version of this page said Blue Bottle "roasts noticeably lighter than Stumptown." Blue Bottle's own labels don't bear that out for its blends: Three Africas is a medium roast, and Bella Donovan and Hayes Valley are dark.

## Who owns them now

Both companies have changed hands, and both changed again in 2026.

**Stumptown.**

- **2015:** Peet's Coffee added Stumptown, along with Intelligentsia, by Peet's own history of itself. Pendergrast writes that Stumptown's lead in nitro cold brew led to the sale.
- **By 2018:** the German holding company JAB owned a majority of Peet's, along with Keurig Green Mountain, Caribou and Jacobs Douwe Egberts, Pendergrast wrote in 2019.
- **Now:** Peet's is one of JDE Peet's three global brands, by JDE Peet's own brand page. On April 1, 2026, Keurig Dr Pepper announced that it had acquired 96.22 percent of JDE Peet's shares, and said it plans to split into two listed companies, one for beverages and one for coffee.

**Blue Bottle.**

- **2017:** Nestlé announced on September 14, 2017, that it had bought a majority stake, with management and employees keeping a minority stake, Bryan Meehan staying on as chief executive and James Freeman as chief product officer. It didn't publish the price or the percentage.
- **2026:** Nestlé said on April 23, 2026, that it had agreed to sell Blue Bottle to Centurium Capital, and its half-year report says that at the end of May it disposed of Blue Bottle, including its activities in the US, China, Hong Kong, Korea and Japan.

So neither is an independent roaster, and the earlier version of this page, which said Stumptown belonged to JAB through Peet's and Blue Bottle to Nestlé, is out of date on both. Whether ownership has changed what's in the bag is a question of taste, and we haven't made a before-and-after comparison; if independence matters to you, it's a reason to look at a local roaster. Our [guide to coffee certifications](/blog/coffee-certifications-decoded/) explains what the labels on a bag do and don't guarantee.

## Where to drink them

Stumptown's locations page, checked October 18, 2026, lists cafés in Portland (including at the airport), New York, the Los Angeles area, where it listed a Lincoln Heights café as opening on Saturday, August 29, and Kyoto, Japan. Blue Bottle's café locator lists cafés in Northern and Southern California, New York, Las Vegas, Chicago, Boston and Washington, D.C., and in Japan, South Korea, mainland China, Hong Kong and Singapore. Both companies also run store finders for their bagged coffee in shops. An earlier version of this page put Stumptown cafés in Chicago and Seattle; its locations page lists neither.

For the cities where each began, and other places to drink specialty coffee, see our [coffee cities guide](/blog/coffee-cities-world-specialty-coffee-travel-guide/).

## Which to try

Going by each company's own descriptions:

- **For chocolate and citrus, or espresso at home:** Hair Bender is Stumptown's espresso blend and its flagship. <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Stumptown+Hair+Bender+Whole+Bean+Coffee&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Stumptown Hair Bender on Amazon</a>.
- **For brighter fruit:** Three Africas, with its two Ethiopian coffees, is the fruitier flagship. <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Blue+Bottle+Three+Africas+Whole+Bean+Coffee&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Blue Bottle Three Africas on Amazon</a>.
- **For an organic everyday blend:** Holler Mountain, with citrus, caramel and berry jam. <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Stumptown+Holler+Mountain+Whole+Bean+Coffee&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Stumptown Holler Mountain on Amazon</a>.

## Brewing them

Blue Bottle publishes a pour-over recipe for its signature blends: 27 g of coffee to 350 g of water, about 1:13, a medium grind, water at 195–205°F&nbsp;(91–96°C), cooler for darker roasts, and four timed pours for a 2:30 to 3:30 brew. That's a stronger ratio than the 1:16 our Brew Guide starts from, so if you want Blue Bottle's own strength, set the Brew Guide to it.
