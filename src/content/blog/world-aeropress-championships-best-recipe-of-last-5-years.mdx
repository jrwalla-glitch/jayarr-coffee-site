The World AeroPress Championship (WAC) is a competition for people who brew with an AeroPress: national, and sometimes regional, championships feed into a World Final, and the WAC says all you need to enter is a genuine AeroPress. After each final, it publishes the podium's recipes, and its recipe page now goes back to 2008.

In 2020 we took the five winning recipes from 2015 to 2019, brewed them back to back with the same beans, and tasted them against a control cup made the maker's way. This page gives each recipe as the WAC publishes it, then how it tasted to us.

## Before you flip: what AeroPress advises

Every one of these five winning recipes uses the inverted method: you stand the AeroPress upside down on its plunger, brew in it, screw on the filter cap, then flip it onto a cup and press. AeroPress itself now says it "strongly discourage[s]" the inverted method because the brewer is less stable that way and prone to tipping over, which can burn you with hot liquid. Brewed as written, each of these recipes means flipping a chamber full of hot coffee, which is the risk AeroPress warns about.

AeroPress's own fix for drips is its Flow Control Filter Cap, a valve that stays shut until you press; the company says the caps were designed to replace the inverted method. The idea has reached the championship too: the WAC lists the 2025 winner, Némo Pop of Australia, brewing upright with a Flow Control Filter Cap.

For temperature, AeroPress suggests 175°F&nbsp;(80°C) for dark roasts and 185°F&nbsp;(85°C) for medium and light roasts, and calls those a starting point. Its how-to page says boiling water is fine if you can't set your kettle. Our [AeroPress guide](/blog/why-and-how-you-should-use-an-aeropress/) gives the maker's upright method in full.

## The recipes at a glance

All five, as the WAC publishes them. The ratio is coffee to all the water, including any added after pressing (calculated).

| Year and champion | Coffee | Brewing water | Added after pressing | Coffee to water |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2019, Wendelien van Bunnik (Netherlands) | 30 g | 100 g at 198°F&nbsp;(92°C) | 100 g, more to taste | 1:6.7 or weaker |
| 2018, Carolina Garay (USA) | 34.9 g | 100 g at 185°F&nbsp;(85°C) | 100 g | 1:5.7 |
| 2017, Paulina Miczka (England) | 35 g | 150 g at 183°F&nbsp;(84°C) | 160–200 g hot water | 1:8.9–1:10 |
| 2016, Filip Kucharczyk (Poland) | 35 g | 150 g at 178°F&nbsp;(81°C) | 100–120 g | 1:7.1–1:7.7 |
| 2015, Lukas Zahradnik (Slovakia) | 20 g | 230 g at 174°F&nbsp;(79°C) | None | 1:11.5 |

For comparison, our Brew Guide's everyday AeroPress recipe is 16 g of coffee to 224 g of water (1:14), brewed upright.

## Our control cup

We started with a control, made as AeroPress's instructions read at the time: one scoop of finely ground coffee, water at 176°F&nbsp;(80°C) to the 1 on the chamber, a 10-second stir, then a gentle press. We used the same beans for the control and all five recipes: a dark roast that, made this way, had a nice richness, a little smoked black pepper, some raisin, perhaps prune and dates, and was smooth and not too bitter.

AeroPress's current instructions differ: 16 to 18 g of coffee, water at 185°F&nbsp;(85°C) poured to the 4, a short stir and a 30-second wait (60 seconds for pre-ground coffee). For a dark roast like ours, though, it still suggests 175°F&nbsp;(80°C).

## The five recipes, in our order

In the video we named our top two outright. The other three are ordered by how the video describes them. Grind sizes are on the WAC's 1-to-10 scale, where 1 is very fine and 10 is very coarse, unless a grinder setting is given.

### 1. Wendelien van Bunnik, 2019 (Netherlands)

Inverted, with a rinsed Aesir paper filter; 30 g of coffee ground at 7 of 10 on a Mahlkönig EK43S; Spa Blauw mineral water (30 ppm) at 198°F&nbsp;(92°C); about 1 minute in the brewer.

1. Pour 100 g of water on the coffee in 10 seconds.
2. Stir firmly 20 times in 10 seconds.
3. Put on the filter cap with its rinsed filter and gently press out the excess air.
4. At 40 seconds, flip the AeroPress and press out all the coffee. You should have about 60 g.
5. Add 100 g of water, then taste and add more until it's the strength you like. (She ended up adding 120 g.)
6. Cool it to about 140°F&nbsp;(60°C) by stirring and pouring it from one vessel to another.

**How it tasted to us:** our favorite. Much more mellow and balanced than the control: the smoke and black pepper hit first, then faded into the fruit, with no bitterness and a lovely aftertaste. It tasted like a leveled-up version of the basic AeroPress cup. Our water was about 194°F&nbsp;(90°C) when we poured, a little under her 198°F&nbsp;(92°C).

### 2. Filip Kucharczyk, 2016 (Poland)

Inverted, with a rinsed paper filter; 35 g of coffee, coarse; water at 178°F&nbsp;(81°C).

1. Start the timer.
2. Pour 150 g of water over 15 seconds.
3. Stir until the timer reads 30 seconds.
4. Put the cap on and wait until the timer reads 1 minute.
5. Flip the AeroPress, give it a swirl and press.
6. Add 100 to 120 g of water, to taste.

**How it tasted to us:** mellow and subdued, like the 2018 cup: not much bitterness, a nice bit of smoke and black pepper, and a little fruit sweetness cutting through. This turned out to be almost exactly the method we use at home: inverted, about 100 g of water, a swirl, press and top up to taste. That's partly why it's our daily choice.

### 3. Carolina Garay, 2018 (USA)

Inverted, with a paper filter rinsed with hot water; 34.9 g of coffee ground at 8 of 10 on a Mahlkönig EK43S; 200 g of Brita-filtered water in all, at 185°F&nbsp;(85°C).

1. Pour 100 g of water over 30 seconds.
2. Stir vigorously but carefully with a pair of wooden chopsticks for 30 seconds.
3. Put the filter cap on, flip the AeroPress and press into a glass server over 30 seconds. Don't preheat the server.
4. Top up with 60 g of water at 185°F&nbsp;(85°C) and 40 g at room temperature.

**How it tasted to us:** mellow and easy to drink straight away, but without as much of the rich dark fruit and smoky, peppery roast we got from the 2019 recipe, and a lot of steps for the result. Our water had cooled to 174°F&nbsp;(79°C) by the time we poured, well under her 185°F&nbsp;(85°C).

### 4. Paulina Miczka, 2017 (England)

Inverted, with a paper filter; 35 g of coffee ground at 8 of 10; water at 183°F&nbsp;(84°C). The WAC lists 370 g of water in all; its steps add up to 310 to 350 g.

1. Put the coffee in the AeroPress.
2. From 0:00 to 0:15, add 150 g of water.
3. From 0:15 to 0:35, stir, and keep stirring.
4. At 0:35, put on the filter cap with a pre-wet filter.
5. At 1:05, flip the AeroPress and start pressing.
6. At 1:35, stop pressing. You should have about 90 ml of concentrate; the WAC notes 4.5% TDS.
7. Add 160 to 200 g of hot water.

**How it tasted to us:** a sweet aroma, then bitter and quite tart. Of the flavors we'd been getting, only the smokiness survived, without the rich dark fruit or the black pepper. Our one-word review was "bitterness". The video doesn't give our water temperature for this one.

### 5. Lukas Zahradnik, 2015 (Slovakia)

Inverted, with a paper filter; 20 g of coffee ground at 7.3 on a Mahlkönig EK43; 230 g of water at 174°F&nbsp;(79°C); 1 minute 35 seconds in all.

1. Add 60 g of water.
2. "Turbulent wiggle" for 15 seconds.
3. Let it bloom for 30 seconds.
4. Add the rest of the water over 10 seconds.
5. Press down for 45 seconds.

**How it tasted to us:** an amazing aroma, but the most potent and bitter of the five, and very peppery. The WAC doesn't say what a turbulent wiggle is, so we improvised one, and since the recipe doesn't say to rinse the filter, we left it dry. We let gravity do most of the 45-second press.

## What the winners had in common

- **All five brewed inverted.** That was common among winners then; the 2025 winner brewed upright with a Flow Control Filter Cap.
- **All five used water below boiling,** from 174 to 198°F&nbsp;(79–92°C), which brackets AeroPress's own 185°F&nbsp;(85°C). That isn't a rule of the competition, though. Later recipes on the WAC's page go hotter: the 2024 winner, George Stanica, brewed at 205°F&nbsp;(96°C), and the 2023 runner-up, Carlo Graf Bülow, at 212°F&nbsp;(100°C). Jonathan Gagné, an astrophysicist who studies brewing, notes in *The Physics of Filter Coffee* that an AeroPress holds its heat so well it brews at almost exactly the kettle's temperature, which is one reason a lower setting matters more here than in a pour-over.
- **Four of five brewed a concentrate and diluted it.** Doses of 30 to 35 g against 100 to 150 g of brewing water make a strong coffee, which water then brings down to drinking strength, as in an americano. Only the 2015 recipe brewed at full volume.
- **Where they rated the grind, it leaned coarse.** The 2018 and 2019 winners put theirs at 8 and 7 on the WAC's 1-to-10 scale, where 10 is very coarse; the 2017 recipe says 8 of 10, and the 2016 recipe says "coarse".
- **Each was built around one coffee.** Finalists brew the championship's official coffee, which the WAC names for 2018 (Mugaya AA from Kirinyaga, Kenya) and 2019 (Finca Buena Vista, El Salvador); the 2024 winner's note on the WAC's page says his method was tailored to that specific coffee and roast. We brewed all five with one dark roast, so our results say how these recipes suited that coffee, not how they'd suit yours.

## Brewing them at home

Start with the 2016 recipe as an everyday method; it's the one we'd actually use daily. When you want something special, try the 2019 recipe, cooling and all. If you'd rather not flip a chamber of hot coffee, AeroPress's upright method and its Flow Control Filter Cap are the maker's answer, and the Brew Guide below brews upright too.
