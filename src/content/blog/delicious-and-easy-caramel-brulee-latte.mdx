The name borrows from crème brûlée, the custard with a caramelized sugar top; Merriam-Webster gives brûlée's French meaning as "scorched." Our quick version gets its caramel from store-bought syrup and sauce. The homemade sauce further down takes the sugar to a deep amber, closer to that scorched-sugar idea.

Our December 2019 video makes the quick version, and the amounts below are the ones on its cards and in its notes. It predates this revision, so the milk temperatures and storage advice here come from the sources named on this page.

## The quick version

**For one latte:**

- **Milk:** 1 cup (8 fl oz, about 240 ml). The video's notes suggest 2 percent, 1 percent or a milk substitute; whole milk works too.
- **Coffee:** 2 shots of espresso, a double, which our Brew Guide makes as about 36 g from 18 g of coffee. Or ⅓ cup (80 ml) of strongly brewed coffee.
- **Caramel syrup:** 1 tablespoon (15 ml, ½ fl oz).
- **Vanilla syrup:** 1 tablespoon (15 ml, ½ fl oz).
- **Caramel sauce,** to drizzle.
- **Makes:** about 10 US fl oz (305 ml) before the foam with espresso, or about 12 fl oz (345 ml) with the brewed coffee, calculated from the volumes above.
- **Time:** about 6 minutes.

**Method:**

1. Heat the milk in a small saucepan over medium heat until hot and steaming, without letting it boil.
2. Pour the hot milk into a French press and pump the plunger up and down for about 30 seconds, until the milk is frothy.
3. Pull 2 shots of espresso into a mug, or pour in ⅓ cup of strong coffee.
4. Stir in the caramel syrup and the vanilla syrup.
5. Pour in the frothed milk, spooning the foam on top.
6. Drizzle caramel sauce over the foam.

**How hot?** If you have a thermometer, the figures for steamed milk are a useful guide in a saucepan too. Breville's Bambino manual has milk ready at 140–149°F&nbsp;(60–65°C). James Hoffmann writes that milk begins to deteriorate in flavor and texture above 154°F&nbsp;(68°C), and that he finds milk heated to 140°F&nbsp;(60°C) better in sweetness and texture than very hot milk. Scott Rao's café target is 150–160°F&nbsp;(66–71°C), and the top of that is past Hoffmann's limit. Whichever you follow, take the pan off before the milk simmers.

**How much foam?** For a latte rather than a cappuccino, Rao's latte recipe has about ¼&nbsp;inch (about 6&nbsp;mm) of foam once the drink settles, against about ½&nbsp;inch for his cappuccino; hold the rest back with a spoon if you like. With a steam wand, our [milk steaming guide](/blog/how-to-steam-milk-for-lattes/) has the method.

## No espresso machine

The video's notes offer ⅓ cup of strongly brewed coffee instead of the shots. A moka pot with a 10 g basket makes about that much.

## Make it more brûlée: a darker caramel sauce

This is our own sauce for the scorched-sugar side of the name. We take the sugar to a deep amber; take it off the heat at light gold if you'd like a milder caramel. It's a small batch on purpose, for the storage reason below.

**Ingredients:**

- **Sugar:** ½ cup (100 g).
- **Hot water:** 2 tablespoons (30 g).
- **Salted butter:** 3 tablespoons (about 43 g).
- **Heavy cream:** ¼ cup (about 60 g).
- **Vanilla extract:** ½ teaspoon.
- **Sea salt:** ⅛ teaspoon.

**Method:**

1. Put the sugar in a heavy saucepan over medium heat. Don't stir; swirl the pan as it melts so it colors evenly.
2. When it's a deep amber, take the pan off the heat.
3. Pour in the hot water slowly and carefully, since it bubbles up hard.
4. Stir in the butter until it melts, then the cream, the vanilla and the salt.
5. Let it cool, then pour it into a clean jar.

It takes about 15 minutes, plus cooling, and starts as about 235 g of ingredients, a little of which steams off. Use 1 to 2 tablespoons in a latte in place of the caramel syrup; it thickens in the fridge, so stir a spoonful into the hot espresso to loosen it.

**Be careful with it.** Melted sugar burns badly and sticks to skin. Keep children away, use a long-handled spoon, and let the sauce cool before you taste it.

**For a crunchy topping of your own** (Starbucks finishes its drink with a "caramel brulée topping"), spoon a thin layer of the amber sugar onto parchment paper before you add the water. It sets hard as it cools, and you can crack it into bits to scatter on the foam.

**How long it keeps.** USDA's FoodKeeper has no entry for homemade caramel sauce. The closest USDA figure is its general guidance for cooked food in the refrigerator: 3 to 4 days. It's a cautious figure for a sauce this sugary, but we haven't found a better-sourced one, so make it in small batches, keep it covered in the fridge at or below 40°F&nbsp;(4°C), and use it within that time.

## What Starbucks puts in its Caramel Brulée Latte

Starbucks describes its Caramel Brulée Latte as its Signature Espresso with steamed milk and "rich caramel brulée sauce," topped with whipped cream and caramel brulée topping, available hot, iced and as a Frappuccino blended beverage. It's a seasonal holiday drink: for 2026, Starbucks says the holiday menu returns, Caramel Brulée Latte included, on November 5.

Our quick version doesn't try to copy Starbucks' sauce. It's a caramel-and-vanilla latte in the same spirit, and the darker sauce gets closer to the scorched-sugar idea in the name.

## Iced, and other swaps

- **Iced.** Stir the syrups into cold brew concentrate, add cold milk and pour it over ice. Skip the frothing.
- **Salted.** Add a pinch of flaky salt on top of the caramel drizzle.
- **Dairy-free.** Use a plant milk made for steaming; our [plant milk guide](/blog/best-plant-milks-for-coffee/) compares them.
