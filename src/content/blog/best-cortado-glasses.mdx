The glass matters to a cortado mostly for three reasons: it has to hold the drink you make with a little room to spare, its wall decides how hot it feels and how you wash it, and its shape can help or hinder pouring milk. This page starts with the drink's size, then compares seven glasses from what their makers publish.

For our March 2026 video, we put eight cortado glasses through five criteria: size for a real cortado, heat retention, build quality, latte art and looks. This revision doesn't rank them on that. It sets out each maker's published capacity, material and care instructions instead, and drops the FLUR glass, because we couldn't find a maker's or authorized seller's page for its cortado glass.

## How big is a cortado?

"Cortado" means cut: espresso cut with a little warm milk. How much milk depends on where you order it. Our Coffee Drinks tool draws each drink from a published recipe:

- **Spain, common style (Café Sabora):** a single shot of about 30 ml with about 10 ml of milk, the 75/25 split Sabora calls common. It says the milk ranges from a few drops to equal parts.
- **Spain, specialty (Ineffable Coffee):** a double, 40 g of espresso with 5 to 40 g of milk, by weight.
- **US chain (Starbucks):** three blonde ristretto shots with steamed whole milk, in an 8 fl oz Short cup.
- **Blue Bottle's Gibraltar:** served in a 4½ oz Libbey Gibraltar glass, 133 ml. Founder James Freeman calls it "really a cortado."

So the Spanish cortados come to about 40 ml, roughly 1⅓ US fl oz, or 45 to 80 g, about 1½ to 2¾ fl oz if you count a gram as a milliliter (both calculated). A glass of 120 to 150 ml leaves room for foam and a steady pour; a 90 ml (3 oz) glass fits the single but is tight for a full double. Makers don't say whether a listed capacity is filled to the brim, so allow some space. Starbucks' version comes in an 8 fl oz cup, so it's a larger drink.

## Single-wall glasses: sturdy and dishwasher-safe

### Duralex Le Picardie

Duralex calls the Picardie the "original French tumbler." It's tempered glass, which Duralex says is 2.5 times stronger than ordinary glass, made in France, stackable, and safe in the microwave, freezer and dishwasher. Duralex USA listed sets of six at $20 for the 9 cl (3 oz) size and $22 for the 13 cl (4⅜ oz). The 3 oz holds a Spanish single; the 4⅜ oz has room for a double. There's nothing cortado-specific about the shape. To buy: Duralex USA, or the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Duralex+Picardie+Tumbler+4+3%2F8+oz&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Duralex Picardie 4⅜ oz</a> on Amazon.

### Libbey Gibraltar

This is the glass the Gibraltar drink is named after. In Blue Bottle's account, someone bought a stock of these 4.5 oz tumblers for its Hayes Valley kiosk in the mistaken belief they'd be big enough for cupping; the baristas used them to check espresso shots instead, then began adding steamed milk to the espresso for regulars. Libbey sells it for cortados as well as whiskey shots and liqueurs: faceted sides, a heavy base, and Libbey's DuraTuff treatment, which it says resists thermal and mechanical shock. It's dishwasher safe, about 3 inches across and 3.1 inches tall, and made in Mexico. Libbey's store listed a set of 12 at $42.99. To buy: Libbey, or the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Libbey+Gibraltar+DuraTuff+Cortado+Glasses+4.5+oz&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Libbey Gibraltar DuraTuff 4.5 oz</a> on Amazon.

### notNeutral Vero Cortado

notNeutral describes its Vero glasses as a take on traditional bar glasses, with exaggerated facets, a satisfying weight and curved interiors "optimized for pouring latte art." The Vero Cortado is hand-pressed, holds 4.25 oz (125 ml), measures 3 inches high and wide, is dishwasher- and microwave-safe, and is made in the USA. notNeutral listed it at $20 a glass, in six colors. To buy: notNeutral, or the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=notNeutral+Vero+Cortado+Glass&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">notNeutral Vero Cortado glass</a> on Amazon.

### Loveramics Twisted Cortado Glass

Designed by Simon Stevens, Loveramics' Urban Glass cortado holds 120 ml (about 4 fl oz). Loveramics lists it at 7.9 × 7.9 × 7.1 cm, 5 mm thick and 236 g, made in China, stackable, and safe in the dishwasher and microwave. Its Hong Kong store listed it at HK$55. Amazon's results for it turn up other brands, so buy it from [Loveramics](https://www.loveramics.com/products/urban-glass-120ml-twisted-cortado-glass-clear) or one of its stockists.

## Double-wall and insulated: mind the care rules

Hearth says its double wall, and KRUVE its insulated glass, keeps the drink warm while the outside stays cool; Fellow builds the Monty with a double wall in ceramic. The trade-off is in the care: check each one before it goes near a dishwasher or microwave.

### Hearth Double-Wall Glass, 4 oz

Hearth describes its 4 oz glass as handmade, kiln-fired, double-walled borosilicate, microwave safe and hand wash only. Espresso Parts, which sells the Hearth line, gives its size as 67 mm tall, 74 mm at the widest and 56 mm across the opening, and listed a set of two at $16.95; Hearth's own store showed it sold out at the same price. Amazon's results for it are other brands, so buy it from [Hearth](https://hearthgoods.com/products/double-wall-glass-coffee-tea-cups) or [Espresso Parts](https://www.espressoparts.com/products/hearth-double-wall-glass-mug-4oz-120ml-clear-set-of-2).

### KRUVE IMAGINE Cortado

KRUVE makes the IMAGINE in four sizes, sold in pairs; the cortado size holds 150 ml (5 oz). KRUVE describes hand-blown, insulated borosilicate glass with a spherical inner wall that keeps a consistent surface area, which it says lets latte art set without distortion. It's dishwasher safe but not microwave safe, because of its gold-leaf logo. KRUVE listed a pair at $42.99. It's the largest glass here, with room for a full Spanish double. To buy: KRUVE, or the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=KRUVE+IMAGINE+Cortado+Glass&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">KRUVE IMAGINE cortado glasses</a> on Amazon.

### Fellow Monty Milk Art Cup, Cortado

The only ceramic cup here, so you won't see the layers. Fellow describes a double-wall ceramic body with a copper or graphite base and a hidden parabolic slope that lifts the crema for latte art. The cortado size holds 4.5 oz and stacks with Fellow's 3 oz demitasse. Fellow says it isn't dishwasher safe, because moisture can get trapped between the double walls, and isn't microwave safe, because of metal parts inside. Fellow listed it at $22.95. To buy: Fellow, or the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Fellow+Monty+Milk+Art+Cortado+Cup&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Fellow Monty cortado cup</a> on Amazon.

## Single or double wall?

- **For daily use and the dishwasher:** the four single-wall glasses here are all dishwasher safe, and the Duralex can also go in the freezer and microwave.
- **For a cooler hand:** Hearth and KRUVE say their glasses insulate. The care rules for these three differ: Hearth asks for hand washing, Fellow warns against the dishwasher, and KRUVE allows it.
- **Warm a heavy single-wall glass first.** A cold, thick glass takes a lot of heat out of a small drink. If an 80 g cortado at 140°F&nbsp;(60°C) went into Loveramics' 236 g glass at 68°F&nbsp;(20°C) and lost no heat to the air, the two would settle at about 113°F&nbsp;(45°C) (calculated, using about 0.84 J/g·K for glass and water's 4.18 J/g·K for the drink). Rinsing the glass with hot water first cuts that loss.
- **For latte art:** the Vero, IMAGINE and Monty all have interiors their makers shaped for pouring; the Picardie and Gibraltar are ordinary tumblers, with no shaped interior claimed.
