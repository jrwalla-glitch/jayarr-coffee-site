A coffee subscription sends freshly roasted beans from independent roasters to your door on a schedule. In March 2020 we compared the two services we saw as the most popular, Bean Box and MistoBox: we received Bean Box's sampler box, tried two dark blends from MistoBox, and rated both. This page keeps that comparison as it was, then sets out what each service offers now, so you can see which of our reasons still apply.

## What we found in 2020

### Bean Box

Bean Box was started by a couple of Seattle-based software engineers, who built a recommendation platform to match coffee drinkers with beans. It worked with small-batch roasters in and around Seattle and across the Pacific Northwest, many of them exclusively. Its selection was smaller than MistoBox's, but distinctive.

We found all of its coffees excellent, both great-tasting and fresh. The standout for us was Caffe Vita's East Timor: earthy and bold, with very pronounced cocoa and caramel notes. The sample box we received was an excellent set of coffees that would be easy to recommend.

The website was easy to use, though less slick-looking than MistoBox's, with a featured-coffee section on varietals, regions and roasts. A dashboard tracked what you'd had and let you rate coffees and leave notes. What set it apart was that a subscription could bring smaller sample bags, so one shipment let you try several coffees instead of committing to one bag. At the time it cost about $24 a month for either the set of four sampler bags or one standard 12 oz bag, as listed then: relatively expensive, but you paid a premium for curation from smaller roasters, and a monthly box of small bags was a rare offer.

### MistoBox

MistoBox worked much like Bean Box, with a few key differences. It offered a much larger range, 614 coffees, many from popular roasters including Onyx, Verve and Intelligentsia, so you weren't likely to run out of new ones to try. A curation engine picked coffees from your answers to a quiz, and the quiz let you choose between blends and single origins. Its interface was noticeably slicker, and it tracked what you'd received and let you like and rate coffees.

Its premium options were limited: you couldn't get a Kona, a Harrar or a Jamaica Blue Mountain from it. We tried two dark blends: Populist Coffee's Dark Lens, bold with notes of cocoa, and the more interesting one, Parisi Artisan Coffee's Italian Blend, which was more like a Sumatra. We preferred Bean Box's coffees a bit, though that depends on what you're sent.

There were no sample boxes, so you went through 12 oz bags one at a time, which is inconvenient if you get one you don't like. Its prices looked a little lower at first, but it charged $5 shipping on each order, as listed then, so overall it ended up priced much like Bean Box.

### Our verdict

Bean Box slightly edged out MistoBox in our ratings, mostly because its sampler bags let you try a bunch of coffees at once: the best option while you're still finding out what you like. MistoBox, with its large number of coffees, might suit a longer-term subscription. With either, you pay a premium for curation and convenience.

Both are still taking subscriptions. Here's what each lists, checked October 18, 2026:

### Bean Box

Bean Box is still based in Seattle, and its About page dates its first shipment to September 2014. It now works with 64 roasters across the US, among them Onyx Coffee Lab, Coava, Portrait and Methodical, plus Seattle-area roasters such as Cloud City, Blossom and Stamp Act, and its head curator, Maryna Gray, selects the coffees.

- **Plans.** Choose a style (Curator's Choice, Single Origin, Light & Bright, Medium & Cozy, Dark & Toasty, Espresso, Decaf or Cold Brew), whole bean or ground, one or two 12 oz bags or one or two 2 lb bags, and a delivery every 2, 3, 4, 5 or 6 weeks. Bean Box lists one 12 oz bag at $19.50 to $21.50 a delivery, and $18 to $20 a bag on two-bag plans. A one-bag Curator's Choice plan came to $24.95 a delivery with the $5.45 flat-rate shipping when checked October 18, 2026, or $17.00 a bag if paid annually.
- **Samplers.** The small-bag sampler that decided our 2020 verdict isn't part of today's subscription plans. Bean Box sells its Coffee Discovery Samplers as one-time gifts, listed at $28.60 to $105.70.
- **Billing and cancelling.** Plans renew automatically and are billed a week before each delivery ships. You can skip, pause, change the frequency or cancel in your account. To avoid the next charge, cancel by the Wednesday before the shipment: Bean Box's pages give that cutoff as 1 p.m. and as 5 p.m. Pacific, so go by the earlier one.
- **Shipping.** Bean Box ships to all 50 states by USPS, straight from the roasters, and says most coffee reaches you 5 to 7 days after roasting. Members get free shipping on separate coffee orders of $39 or more from a single roaster, as listed when checked; plan deliveries keep their flat rate.

To start a plan: [beanbox.com](https://beanbox.com/coffee-subscription/configure).

### Misto, formerly MistoBox

MistoBox now calls itself Misto on its website, which is still at mistobox.com, under Mistobox, LLC. On November 11, 2020, eight months after our video, Portland-based Clive Coffee announced it had completed the acquisition of a majority interest in it, as Daily Coffee News reported the same day; MistoBox's co-founder and CEO, Connor Riley, joined Clive's management team and kept a stake in the business. MistoBox launched in 2012, pitched on *Shark Tank* in 2013 and went on to strike a deal with Mark Cuban.

- **Plans.** A short quiz about how you brew and what you like, then an algorithm matches coffees from more than 60 roasters, among them Cat & Cloud, Ceremony, Coava, Equator, Joe Coffee and Sightglass. The Brew Queue lets you line up coffees yourself, and you can rate what you receive. Misto listed bags from $20 with free shipping when checked October 18, 2026.
- **Price control.** You set a maximum price per coffee; roasters set their own prices, so a higher maximum opens more coffees. Prepaid plans add bonus credit: $105 of credit for $100, $165 for $150 and $230 for $200, as listed.
- **Pausing and cancelling.** You can cancel any time from your dashboard, or push your next shipment out by up to 90 days instead. A prepaid balance renews automatically until you cancel; any credit left stays usable.
- **Shipping.** Coffee ships from the roaster, and Misto's FAQ counts bags of 10, 11 and 12 oz. Its site doesn't say where it ships.

To start: [mistobox.com](https://mistobox.com/subscribe).

### What's changed since our verdict

Our 2020 reasons have partly reversed. The sampler bags that tipped our verdict to Bean Box are now a one-time gift rather than a subscription option, and shipping has switched sides: in 2020 MistoBox's $5 an order, as listed then, brought it level with Bean Box on price, while now Bean Box adds a flat rate to each plan delivery and Misto ships free. Both now offer a catalog of more than 600 coffees. If you want to explore with small bags, buy a Bean Box sampler once and then subscribe to whichever service carries the roasters you liked.

## How much coffee you need

Plan your delivery size from what you drink. A 12 oz bag is 340 g. Our Brew Guide's V60 for a 12 fl oz (355 ml) mug uses 25.4 g of coffee at 1:16, so one bag makes about 13 such mugs, and two mugs a day use about a bag a week (both calculated). The services count more cups because they count smaller ones: Bean Box says a 12 oz bag brews 24 to 36 cups, and Misto about 24, counting 8 oz cups at about 13.3 g of coffee each, which it gives as the Specialty Coffee Association's ratio.

So for one 12 oz mug a day, a 12 oz bag every two weeks is about right; for two a day, two bags every two weeks. If you'd rather run short than have coffee pile up, start smaller and add a bag.

## Keeping subscription coffee fresh

Whole beans usually taste best about 7 to 21 days after roasting and stay good to about day 30, typical windows rather than a forecast for your bag. If a bag reaches you 5 to 7 days after roasting, as Bean Box says most of its coffee does, finishing it within two weeks keeps it inside that usual window (calculated). Keep it sealed in its bag or an airtight, opaque container somewhere cool and dark, not in the fridge, and freeze what you won't use in time. Our guides to [storing](/blog/how-to-store-coffee-beans/) and [freezing](/blog/how-to-freeze-coffee-beans/) beans explain how.

## Before you subscribe

- **A roast date on every bag,** so you can see how fresh it is when it arrives.
- **Shipping straight from the roaster** soon after roasting, as both of these services describe.
- **Easy skip, pause and cancel online,** and know the cutoff before each charge.
- **A frequency that matches what you drink,** from the arithmetic above.
- **The full price of each delivery,** shipping included.

Once you're receiving coffee, rate what you get: both services use your ratings to choose what comes next. Our [coffee-to-water ratio guide](/blog/coffee-to-water-ratio-guide/) and [grind size guide](/blog/coffee-grind-size-guide-what-grind-for-every-brew-method/) help you brew it well.
