Some coffee traditions add spice. In parts of Ethiopia, Jeff Koehler writes, wild cardamom or a clove is sometimes pounded with the roasted beans, a curl of cinnamon bark near the Sudan border, or fresh wild ginger in Kafa; our [Ethiopian coffee ceremony guide](/blog/ethiopian-coffee-ceremony-explained/) has more. In Mexico, café de olla is brewed with cinnamon and piloncillo. Not every coffee tradition uses spice, though: Türkiye's registered description of Turkish coffee, and UNESCO's description of it, name only coffee, water and sugar.

This page is about adding spice: how, how much, and four recipes. The amounts are our starting points, not anyone's standard, so adjust them to taste.

Our March 2020 video is about a different kind of spicy coffee: coffees whose own flavor has spice notes, with no spice added. That's covered [near the end of this page](#when-coffee-tastes-spicy-on-its-own).

## Three ways to add spice

- **In the grounds.** Mix ground or crushed whole spice into the coffee before you add the water, and it brews with the coffee. Our recipe below does this in a French press, where the spice sits in the water for the whole steep.
- **In the cup.** Stir a pinch of ground spice into the finished coffee. It's the quickest way to try a spice, but ground spice doesn't dissolve, so some settles at the bottom of the cup.
- **Simmered.** Heat whole spices in the water, then add the coffee, as café de olla does. It takes longer and makes a sweet, spiced pot to share.

## How much of each spice

Our starting amounts for a 12 oz mug's worth of coffee:

| Spice | Start with | Best added |
|---|---|---|
| Cinnamon | ¼ tsp ground, or half a stick | In the grounds, or simmered |
| Cardamom | 1 green pod, crushed, or a pinch ground | In the grounds |
| Ginger | ⅛ tsp ground, or a thin slice of fresh | In the grounds or the cup |
| Nutmeg | A small pinch, freshly grated | In the cup or the milk |
| Clove | 1 whole clove, or a tiny pinch ground | In the grounds, or simmered |
| Star anise | 1 point broken from a pod | In the grounds, or simmered |
| Black pepper | A few grinds | In the grounds |
| Cayenne | A tiny pinch, about 1/16 tsp | In the cup |

Start at the low end, especially with clove, star anise and cayenne, and try one spice at a time before you combine them.

**Keeping spices.** USDA's FoodKeeper gives ground spices 2 to 3 years in the pantry for best quality, and whole spices 3 to 4 years. Whole spices, ground or crushed as you need them, last longer.

## Our spiced French press

Our own recipe, built on our Brew Guide's French press for a 12 oz mug, which follows Jessica Easto's eight-minute method:

- **Coffee:** 27.3 g, ground extra coarse.
- **Spice:** ¼ teaspoon ground cinnamon and 1 green cardamom pod, crushed.
- **Water:** 409 g at 205°F&nbsp;(96°C), a ratio of 1:15.
- **Makes:** about 12 US fl oz (355 ml), since the grounds hold back about 2 g of water per gram of coffee, the figure the Brew Guide uses.
- **Time:** about 10 minutes, 8 of them steeping.

1. Warm the French press, then add the coffee, the cinnamon and the crushed cardamom pod, and stir them together.
2. Start a timer and pour in all 409 g of water, wetting all the grounds.
3. At 0:30, stir gently to sink the crust.
4. Put the lid on with the plunger up and leave it until 8:00.
5. Press gently and pour it all out straight away.

## Café de olla

Café de olla is Mexico's "pot coffee." USDA's Mexico City office describes it as roasted coffee prepared with cinnamon and sweetened, sold at breakfast time from street tricycles in Mexico City and other cities. El Universal, citing *Claridades Agropecuarias*, traces it to the Mexican Revolution, when, it says, the *adelitas* mixed coffee in water with spices to lift the fighters' spirits.

This is El Universal's recipe, in its own amounts:

- **Water:** 6 cups (about 1,420 ml, calculated).
- **Piloncillo:** one 200 g piece. Piloncillo is unrefined cane sugar; if you can't find it, dark brown sugar is our stand-in.
- **Cinnamon:** 2 sticks.
- **Cloves:** 2.
- **Ground coffee:** 6 tablespoons.

1. Put the water in a pot. El Universal recommends a clay pot (*olla de barro*), but a saucepan works.
2. Add the piloncillo, cinnamon and cloves, and stir until the piloncillo dissolves.
3. Boil it for 5 minutes.
4. Add the coffee, turn the heat down and keep it hot, without boiling, for 5 minutes, stirring.
5. Strain it, or let the grounds settle for a moment, and serve.

By weight, it's a light coffee. Six tablespoons of ground coffee is about 42 g, at about 7 g per tablespoon (the figure in Nguyen Coffee Supply's phin recipe), so the pot runs about 1:34 coffee to water by weight before any boils off (calculated). That's far more water per gram of coffee than a 1:15 French press. For a stronger pot, add more coffee; for a less sweet one, use less piloncillo.

## Cardamom coffee in a cezve

Turkish coffee's registered method uses no spice, but cardamom is an easy addition. Follow our [Turkish coffee guide](/blog/turkish-coffee-guide/) for one small cup, 6 g of extra-fine coffee to 60 ml of cold water, and add 1 green cardamom pod, crushed, or a small pinch of ground cardamom with the coffee. Heat it slowly and take it off as the foam starts to form, before it boils. The cardamom is our variation, not part of the registered recipe.

## Ginger and cayenne, stirred in

For one 8 oz cup of hot coffee, stir in ¼ teaspoon of ground ginger, a tiny pinch of cayenne (about 1/16 teaspoon), ½ teaspoon of honey and a splash of cream. Stir again before the last sip, since the ground spice settles. Cayenne brings heat, so add it a little at a time.

## A spiced latte

Brew spiced coffee by any method above, or stir a pinch of cinnamon and cardamom into espresso, then add steamed milk and a little honey. ## When coffee tastes spicy on its own

On a bag of unflavored coffee, spice notes aren't added spice: they describe the coffee itself, and they're what our video is about. World Coffee Research's Sensory Lexicon, which WCR says is the basis for the revised Coffee Taster's Flavor Wheel, groups them under spices:

- **Brown spice:** "the sweet, brown aromatic associated with spices such as cinnamon, clove, nutmeg, and allspice," with cinnamon, clove, nutmeg and anise as attributes of their own.
- **Pepper:** the aromatic "characteristic of ground black pepper."
- **Pungent:** "a sharp, physically penetrating sensation in the nasal cavity."

A note like "cinnamon" on a bag describes what the roaster's tasters found in that coffee, so treat it as what the coffee *can* taste like, not a promise. To learn to pick these notes out, smell the spice itself, then sip the coffee and breathe out through your nose.
