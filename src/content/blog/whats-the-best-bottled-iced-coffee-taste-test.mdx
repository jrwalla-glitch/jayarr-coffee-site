In July 2020 we tasted eight bottled iced coffees and scored each out of 10: four from Starbucks, three from SToK and a can of La Colombe. We judged flavor, body, smoothness and balance, and watched for the two faults that sank most of them: a watery cup and a bitter edge. Before tasting, we lined the bottles up in the order we expected to like them, going on looks and brand alone.

## Our 2020 scores

These are the scores written on the cups at the end of the video. An earlier version of this page had four of them wrong (the SToK Protein, Starbucks Medium Roast, La Colombe and Starbucks Blonde Roast); the table now matches the video.

| Bottle | Score | What we noted |
|---|---|---|
| SToK Chocolate Black cold brew | 9 | Not too sweet, with no milk and 70 calories on the 2020 label, yet it tasted like a mocha: strong coffee first, then a lingering chocolate finish. Smooth, with no acidity, and not watery. |
| SToK Protein Cold Brew | 9 | Creamy and very smooth, with a light coffee flavor, about twice the chocolate one's calories and no chalky aftertaste. A good pick around a workout. |
| Starbucks Iced Espresso, Caffè Mocha | 8 | Rich and chocolaty, with no bitterness. A little too sweet: almost a dessert. The highest-calorie bottle we tried. |
| Starbucks Dark Roast Unsweetened Iced Coffee | 7 | Dark but not bitter: rich and full without being heavy. The best black Starbucks bottle. |
| SToK Un-Sweet Black cold brew | 7 | Very smooth, with no acidity or bitterness. It would have scored 9 if it weren't noticeably watery. |
| Starbucks Medium Roast Unsweetened Iced Coffee | 5 | Good flavor and not watery, but a little strong and too bitter. |
| La Colombe Triple Draft Latte (can) | 5 | Smooth, creamy and strong, but a little watery, with an acidic, bitter aftertaste that lingered. |
| Starbucks Blonde Roast Unsweetened Iced Coffee | 4 | A light, refreshing blonde flavor, but watery, with a bitter acidity. Our lowest score. |

## The big surprise

Going on packaging and brand, we expected Starbucks' Medium Roast to be the best, and put SToK, a brand we didn't know, further down the line. SToK took both top spots. Starbucks' Blonde Roast, which we had lined up second, came last. Don't judge a bottled coffee by its label.

A few things the labels show:

- **Starbucks' black bottles are iced coffee, not cold brew.** Each lists one ingredient: "brewed Starbucks coffee (water, coffee)." SToK's Un-Sweet Black is cold brew, and lists "coffee (filtered water, coffee), natural flavor." SToK's FAQ puts its cold brews at about 120–170 mg of caffeine a serving.
- **The milky ones lead with milk.** The Caffè Mocha's ingredients start with reduced-fat milk, then brewed Starbucks espresso coffee, sugar, skim milk and cocoa. La Colombe says its Triple Draft Latte is made with "espresso roast cold brew" and milk, and lists whole milk, coffee and cane sugar.
- **La Colombe has cut the caffeine.** It calls 140 mg its "new reduced caffeine level" and says cans from older stock may still turn up, so a can you buy may not match its page.

The ingredient list won't tell you how strong a bottle is. US labels list ingredients "in descending order of predominance by weight," under the FDA's labeling rule, so the first ingredient is simply what there's most of: the coffee itself, which is mostly water, or the milk. The caffeine figure says more about how much coffee went in, and all four makers here publish it.

## Caffeine: bottles against a homemade glass

Per 12 fl oz serving, the black bottles run from 120 mg (Starbucks Blonde Roast) to 220 mg (Starbucks Dark Roast). For comparison, our Caffeine Calculator estimates about 155–330 mg for a 12 fl oz glass of homemade cold brew made from the Brew Guide's concentrate and mixed one to one, a wide range because beans and extraction vary.

The long steep isn't what makes cold brew strong. Megan Fuller and Niny Rao found that caffeine in their cold brews leveled off after about 6 to 7 hours. A glass carries more caffeine when more coffee went into it, as with a concentrate, or when the glass is bigger. Our [caffeine guide](/blog/caffeine-in-coffee-guide/) has the FDA's and EFSA's guidance on daily amounts.

## Bottled or homemade?

Homemade cold brew takes more coffee than you might expect. With Jessica Easto's concentrate, 96 g of coffee to 600 g of water, mixed one to one, a 12 fl oz glass takes about 42 g of coffee, so a 12 oz (340 g) bag makes about eight glasses (calculated with our Brew Guide). Easto keeps the concentrate in an airtight container in the fridge for one to two weeks; our [cold brew concentrate guide](/blog/cold-brew-concentrate-guide/) has the recipe, the storage research and whether cold brew is really less acidic.
