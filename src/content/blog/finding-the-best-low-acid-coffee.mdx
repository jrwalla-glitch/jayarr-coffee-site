If you find yourself reaching for the antacid after your morning cup, you're not alone. After testing seven low acid coffees for our 2020 video and reading the research since, the clearest lesson is that "acidic" means three different things, and the label only promises one of them.

## Three meanings of "acidic"

**pH** measures the free hydrogen ions in the cup at that moment. Brewed coffee is mildly acidic: in Niny Rao and Megan Fuller's 2018 study of six coffees, hot and cold brews all measured between pH 4.85 and 5.13. Each pH unit is a tenfold step, so small-looking differences are bigger than they seem.

**Titratable acidity** counts all the acid a base can neutralize, including acid molecules that haven't released their hydrogen yet. Two coffees can share a pH and still differ in total acid, which is what Rao and Fuller found with cold brew (below).

**Perceived acidity** is taste: the brightness a roaster praises, or the sourness you notice when a brew runs short. In UC Davis tests of drip coffee, Mackenzie Batali and colleagues found titratable acidity was an adequate predictor of how sour tasters found a cup, while pH showed little relationship with how strong or how extracted the coffee was.

A "low acid" label almost always means pH. Puroast, for example, lists its House Blend at pH 5.7 to 5.9 against 4.8 to 5.0 for conventional coffee. Our calculation: that gap means roughly 5 to 13 times fewer free hydrogen ions. It doesn't tell you the coffee's titratable acidity, how bright it tastes, or how your stomach will respond.

## Roast level changes the acids, not the guarantee

Roasting breaks down chlorogenic acids. Moon, Yoo and Shibamoto measured total chlorogenic acid falling to about half after their lightest roast, 12 minutes at 446°F&nbsp;(230°C), and to almost trace levels after their darkest, 21 minutes at 482°F&nbsp;(250°C). They also saw a general correlation between these acids and pH. In the cup, James Hoffmann writes that "it is generally agreed" that the longer a coffee is roasted, the less acidity it ends up with, while bitterness rises.

That's chemistry and taste, not a promise about your stomach:

- In a small study of nine healthy volunteers, Rubach and colleagues found a dark-roast blend stimulated less stomach acid secretion than a medium-roast market blend. The dark blend had more N-methylpyridinium and less chlorogenic acid, and the authors called for further studies before drawing conclusions.
- In John DiBaise's 2003 randomized, double-blind crossover trial, 30 coffee-sensitive people drank a conduction-roasted coffee and a differently processed but otherwise similar one. Both coffees brought on heartburn, regurgitation or indigestion in most people, with no significant difference between them, fasting or after a meal.

So a darker roast will usually taste less bright, but neither roast level nor a "low acid" label guarantees a lower acid measurement or fewer symptoms.

## Cold brew: same pH, less total acid

Rao and Fuller brewed six light-roast coffees two ways: cold (7 hours at room temperature) and hot (6 minutes with boiling water). The pH values were comparable, from 4.85 to 5.13. Titratable acidity was lower in every cold brew: by our calculation from their table, between about 10% and 40% lower, depending on the coffee and the titration endpoint.

They didn't measure stomach symptoms, and in their conclusion they describe the gentler-on-the-stomach idea as a claim made by cold brew enthusiasts. Cold brew does give you a cup with less total acid; whether that matters for your stomach is a separate, untested question. Our [cold brew tutorial](/blog/whats-great-about-cold-brew-toddy-cold-brew-tutorial/) covers making it.

## Origin and processing: perceived acidity

Some origins and processes tend to taste less bright. In *The World Atlas of Coffee*, Hoffmann describes better Brazilian coffees as tending to be "low in acidity, heavy in body and sweet", and Indonesia's semi-washed (giling basah) coffees as heavy-bodied and earthy "with very little acidity". [Brazilian](/blog/is-brazilian-coffee-any-good/) and Indonesian coffees are a reasonable place to start if you want a cup that tastes less bright. That's taste, not a pH figure.

If a cup tastes sharply sour rather than pleasantly bright, the brew may be the cause rather than the beans.

## What health agencies say

The US National Institute of Diabetes and Digestive and Kidney Diseases (NIDDK) puts it this way: "Some people who have GERD find that certain foods or drinks trigger symptoms or make symptoms worse." Its list of foods and drinks "commonly linked to GERD symptoms" includes "coffee and other sources of caffeine", and it notes that "your doctor may recommend reducing or avoiding certain foods or drinks to see if GERD symptoms improve."

That's population guidance, not a plan for you. If coffee seems to set off heartburn or reflux, ask your clinician before relying on a "low acid" coffee to manage it.

## Seven low acid coffees, tasted

Six of these come from our 2020 video; Teeccino, an herbal alternative, was added in a later update. The tasting notes are ours; the claims are the makers' own, as published on their websites when checked on October 15, 2026. None of the makers' acidity claims shows that a coffee will prevent heartburn.

### <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Volcanica+Low+Acid+Coffee&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Volcanica Low Acid Coffee</a>

**As published by the maker:** a medium roast with "a measured pH of 5.3", made from "beans from Brazil, Sumatra, and other Arabica coffees grown at lower altitudes" using what Volcanica calls a proprietary roasting method.

**What we found:** our pick in the video. It was smooth, with a light mouthfeel and no astringency, and it tasted like ordinary good coffee without the bite; the Brazilian and Sumatran parts can bring chocolate and nut notes. Watch the brew time so it doesn't over-extract.

### <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Lifeboost+Grata+Medium+Roast+Coffee&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Lifeboost Grata Medium Roast</a>

**As published by the maker:** listed as Grata Medium Roast, a "low-acid" single-origin Arabica grown in South America, "USDA certified organic" and "third-party tested for mycotoxins, heavy metals, pesticides". The site gives no pH figure.

**What we found:** exceptionally clean, though some may find it slightly muted next to brighter coffees.

### <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Mommee+Coffee+Half+Caf&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Mommee Coffee Half Caf</a>

**As published by the maker:** "50mg caffeine per cup" (the site doesn't give the cup size), a decaf it describes as "Mountain Water process", and a roast it says is "lower in Chlorogenic Acid".

**What we found:** a slightly burnt quality with some bitterness.

Mommee aims its coffees at new and expecting mothers. The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists' committee opinion says that "moderate caffeine consumption (less than 200 mg per day) does not appear to be a major contributing factor in miscarriage or preterm birth." If you're pregnant or breastfeeding, ask your clinician what's right for you.

### Franck's Coffee Peruvian Dark

A blend of roasted Arabica and roasted flax seed when we tasted it. It was pleasant, nutty and buttery, but it doesn't taste like ordinary coffee. We couldn't find a maker's page for it on October 15, 2026, so we can't confirm its current recipe, and there's no link.

### <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Puroast+Low+Acid+House+Blend+Coffee&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Puroast Low Acid House Blend</a>

**As published by the maker:** a medium roast, slow-roasted "at lower temperatures using a biomass-fueled furnace", with a pH of 5.7 to 5.9 that Puroast says North Carolina A&T State University verified independently. (The pH arithmetic is above.)

**What we found:** darker and more bitter-tasting than the others, and not the most nuanced cup.

### <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Teeccino+French+Roast+Chicory+Herbal+Coffee&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Teeccino French Roast Chicory Herbal Coffee</a>

Not coffee at all. **As published by the maker:** organic carob, barley, chicory and ramón seeds with natural coffee flavor, "Caffeine Free", with "no chlorogenic acid".

**What we found:** it doesn't taste like coffee. It tastes like an herbal drink that reminds you of coffee, so treat it as a different drink, not a stand-in for coffee.

### Healthwise Low Acid Coffee

Consistent and without surprises when we tasted it. Healthwise's website didn't load on October 15, 2026, so we can't confirm the maker's current claims or how to buy it, and there's no link.
