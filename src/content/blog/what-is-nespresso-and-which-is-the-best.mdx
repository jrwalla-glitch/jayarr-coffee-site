A Nespresso machine makes a cup from a sealed, pre-ground capsule at the press of a button: no grinder, no scale, no tamping. The choice that matters most comes before any machine, because Nespresso sells two systems, Original and Vertuo, and a capsule made for one won't work in the other.

## Original or Vertuo: decide this first

Nespresso's US help pages call Vertuo "a completely separate system" from Original: because of differences in size, shape and design, Vertuo capsules won't work in Original machines, "and vice-versa."

| | Original | Vertuo |
|---|---|---|
| How it brews | A pump pushes water through the capsule | The machine reads a barcode on the capsule and spins it |
| Cup sizes | 25 to 110 ml | 25 to 230 ml; 355 ml on newer machines |
| Capsules in Nespresso's US range, October 15, 2026 | 45 | 74 |
| Capsules from other brands | Some brands make capsules for Original machines | Nespresso's own range includes Starbucks-branded Vertuo capsules |

Original capsules make a ristretto (25 ml, about 0.85 US fl oz), an espresso (40 ml, 1.35 fl oz) or a lungo (110 ml, 3.7 fl oz). Vertuo capsules make an espresso (40 ml), a double espresso (80 ml, 2.7 fl oz), a gran lungo (150 ml, 5.1 fl oz), a mug (230 ml, 7.8 fl oz) or, on newer machines, a 355 ml (12 fl oz) cup, and Nespresso sells one 25 ml Vertuo ristretto. The fluid ounces are calculated. The sizes and capsule counts are from our Pod Explorer's data, which follows Nespresso's US catalog as checked October 15, 2026; the counts include limited and seasonal editions but not carafe capsules or retired and unlisted ones.

Nespresso's VertuoPlus page says the barcode sets the brewing parameters for each capsule and calls the spinning "Centrifusion." The Original machines in our chooser that publish a pump rating list 19 bar, as published by their makers.

Two details catch people out:

- **Not every Vertuo machine takes the 355 ml capsules.** Nespresso lists the XL Alto and Cold Brew Style capsules for the Vertuo Creatista, Lattissima, Next, Pop and Pop+ and Up, not the original Vertuo, Evoluo or VertuoPlus. The Vertuo Next's 2025 manual also lists a 535 ml (18 fl oz) carafe size; the Pod Explorer leaves carafe capsules out.
- **Other brands' Original capsules come with their own compatibility lists.** Peet's, for example, says its espresso capsules fit Original machines including the Essenza Mini, Essenza Plus, Pixie, CitiZ, Lattissima, KitchenAid and Creatista, and don't fit Vertuo machines, so that Creatista is the Original model, not the Vertuo Creatista. Check the capsule maker's list against your machine before you buy a box.

## What the intensity number means, and caffeine

Nespresso's intensity scale is not a caffeine scale. Nespresso's US help pages define intensity as "a combination of bitterness, body and roastiness" and say it "is not based on caffeine content."

For caffeine, the same pages give these ranges, which vary because coffee is a natural product:

- **Espressos:** most contain 50 to 100 mg per cup.
- **Vertuo coffees in the 7.77 oz (230 ml) mug size:** 170 to 200 mg per cup.
- **Decaffeinated capsules:** less than 0.1% caffeine, which Nespresso puts at 3 to 6 mg per cup. Decaf isn't caffeine-free.

Our [caffeine guide](/blog/caffeine-in-coffee-guide/) compares these with other ways of making coffee.

## Is it espresso?

It depends on what you mean by espresso. An Original machine pushes hot water through the coffee with a pump, as an espresso machine does. A Vertuo machine spins the capsule instead, and makes both espresso sizes and mug-size cups.

What you give up is control. Our Brew Guide's espresso starts at 18 g of coffee to a 36 g shot in 25 to 30 seconds, and you choose the beans, the grind and the dose. With a capsule, those are set for you; you choose the capsule and the cup size, and on some machines the volume. Nespresso's help pages note that its machines have no manual temperature control, apart from the Expert and Creatista models. What you gain is a repeatable cup at the press of a button, with no grinder to buy.

## Choosing a machine

Once you know the system, the next questions are milk and budget. This table is a research comparison from the makers' own pages, as recorded in our Espresso Machine Chooser's data, checked October 15, 2026; specs are as published by the maker.

| Machine (system) | Milk | Heat-up | Listed price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vertuo Next (Vertuo) | None | 15 s | About $180 |
| VertuoPlus (Vertuo) | None | 40 s | About $200 |
| Vertuo Creatista (Vertuo) | Automatic steam wand | 30 s | About $700 |
| Pixie (Original) | None | 25 s | About $240 |
| CitiZ (Original) | None | 25 s | About $320 |
| Lattissima One (Original) | Automatic milk system, with a 4.2 oz jug | – | About $430 |

- **A dash** means the chooser's data has no figure.
- **The VertuoPlus can't take the 355 ml XL capsules**, by Nespresso's list above; the Vertuo Next and Creatista can.
- **Machines without milk** need a separate frother for lattes. The Vertuo Next's manual points owners to Nespresso's Aeroccino frothers.
- **Prices** are what each maker's US site showed on October 15, 2026: Breville's for the Vertuo machines, De'Longhi's for the Lattissima One, and Nespresso's for the Pixie and CitiZ. They change often.

## Looking after it

Descale as your machine's manual says. Nespresso's US help pages give a general interval of every three months or 300 capsules, whichever comes first. Some machines tell you: the Vertuo Next blinks orange three times when it needs descaling, and its manual's procedure takes about 20 minutes with Nespresso's descaling kit.
