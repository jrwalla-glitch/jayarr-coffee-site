Alignment problems are real, but they're one cause among several for an uneven grind, and the fix means taking the burrs apart. This guide separates the check from the repair, and sets out what makers and published sources say about each.

## What alignment means

Barista Hustle wrote in 2017 that grinding burrs "need to be parallel, and concentrically and radially aligned to work properly", and that a misalignment of about 0.05 mm (50 µm) noticeably changes the coffee a grinder makes.

Scott Rao, in *Espresso Extraction*, describes three structural problems he has met in grinders:

1. **Divergent burrs,** the most common in his experience. It usually happened when a barista reseated the burrs after cleaning: one or two ground particles left under a burr can tilt it enough to degrade the grind.
2. **Burrs not lined up with each other.** Some professional grinders hold their burrs with screws in holes wider than the screws, so a burr can sit 1 to 2 mm off center from its partner.
3. **A bent motor shaft,** which he found only when a friend with a machine shop measured the wobble.

How common this is varies by grinder. Rao estimated that at most 10% of the more than 100 Mahlkönig EK grinders he had used were well aligned out of the box. Turin, by contrast, doesn't think alignment is needed in most cases on its own grinders.

Alignment also muddies grinder comparisons. In his 2023 analysis of 24 espresso grinders, Jonathan Gagné cautions that there was no way of knowing whether the grinders measured were well aligned, which could plausibly affect how evenly they ground.

## Is it alignment? Check the simple things first

Turin's troubleshooting guide lists four suspects for an inconsistent grind or wild shot times. Work through them from the cheapest to check:

- **New burrs still seasoning.** See [below](#alignment-isnt-seasoning-or-wear).
- **Retention and static.** Purge the grinder and clean the chute; Turin also suggests a drop or two of water on the beans, the [Ross Droplet Technique](/blog/rdt-ross-droplet-technique-grinding/).
- **Worn or chipped burrs.** Inspect the edges.
- **Alignment.**

Before any of these, clean the burr faces and the surfaces they sit on, since grounds trapped under a burr can tilt it. For a scraping noise, Turin points to grounds or a pinched burr seal under the burr, or a carrier that isn't fully seated. And if a burr seems to wobble as it spins, Turin says that on many grinders, its own included, the screw holding the lower carrier creates the illusion; cover the screw and watch the outer edge.

## Check your maker's rules first

Some makers align the burrs at the factory and tell you not to touch the mounts. Others leave it to you. Here is what each maker says, checked October 15, 2026, with its exact words in quotation marks:

| Grinder | What the maker says |
|---|---|
| Comandante C40 | Don't unscrew the three mounting screws around the burr: they are tightened by hand at the factory to calibrate the grinder, and undoing them "can damage your grinder and void warranty". Some burr contact at fine settings is normal, because the inner cone "floats" on the axle. |
| Timemore Chestnut C | "It's not recommended that customers disassemble or replace any part of it." |
| 1Zpresso JX-Pro | Don't take apart the central shaft and burr, the burr ring unit and outer burr, or the bearings. 1Zpresso says its grinders come apart for cleaning "without re-calibrating the burr". |
| Kinu M47 Classic | Kinu describes its burrs as "perfectly aligned, auto-centered", thanks to a Morse-cone design. |
| Turin DF64 Gen 2 | Alignment isn't needed in most cases; if you want it as close to perfect as possible, do a marker test and shim if necessary. Aftermarket SSP burrs "require installation and alignment". |
| Niche Zero | The manual covers cleaning and recalibrating the dial's zero mark, not burr alignment. |

So for the hand grinders here, alignment is the maker's job. The marker test is mainly for flat-burr electrics like the DF64, especially after a burr swap. If your grinder isn't listed, read its manual and warranty first.

## The marker test: a check, not a fix

Turin points DF64 owners to the marker test, and a 2019 guest post on Scott Rao's blog describes marker alignment as a method that relies on the user's own observations. The idea is simple: ink rubs off wherever two burr faces touch.

## Shimming: the invasive part

Shimming means putting thin shim material under the burr on the side that didn't touch, to raise it, then repeating the marker test. Do it only where your maker allows it. A few rules from the makers and published sources:

- **One change at a time.** Shim, reassemble and test again before adding more.
- **Tighten evenly.** Turin recommends a cross pattern, a little on each screw until all are firmly tight, and warns that overtightening can strip the screws or the aluminum screw holes.
- **Uneven seating.** Turin notes that unevenly tightened screws can leave the burrs out of line. Its advice is to loosen the screws slightly and shift the burr back and forth while retightening each screw a little at a time, round and round.
- **Expect to repeat it.** Rao wrote in 2019 that home realignment methods take an hour or two each time the burrs are removed or replaced.

His blog also hosted a more permanent method for EK43-style grinders: sanding the burr mounting surfaces flat to the drive shaft. Rao expected it to take three to four hours but not to need repeating, and its author, Mitchell Hale, warns that it changes the grinder's zero significantly. Rao hadn't tried it, and told readers to try it at their own risk. A bent shaft is beyond any of this: Rao needed a machine shop to find his.

## Alignment isn't seasoning, or wear

New burrs change as they wear in, and that can look like an alignment problem:

- Niche says the Niche Zero grinds well from the start and reaches peak consistency after about 10 kg of coffee.
- Turin says new burrs need about 10 lb (4.5 kg, calculated) to stabilize, and recommends using the grinder normally rather than wasting coffee on seasoning.
- In Gagné's 2019 measurements, his new burrs ground coarser at first, then more evenly, over 24 lb of coffee; he couldn't say whether that holds for all burrs. More in our [burr seasoning](/blog/burr-seasoning-grind-quality/) post.

Worn burrs are a different problem again. Rao writes that as burrs dull, they make both more fines and more boulders, and the net result is always slower flow and less extraction. Makers rate burr life differently: Eureka gives 300 kg for the Mignon Silenzio 55's burrs, and Niche says the Niche Zero's last more than 750 kg. Alignment can't restore a dull edge.

## After any change, find your zero again

Reseating, shimming or sanding can all move the point where the burrs touch, so re-find zero and dial in again from your maker's starting setting.
