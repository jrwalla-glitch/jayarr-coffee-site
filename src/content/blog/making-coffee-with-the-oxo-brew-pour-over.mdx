Most pour-over drippers leave the pouring to you. OXO's takes it over: you fill a tank, and a pattern of holes in its base lets the water down onto the grounds at a set rate. That makes it one of the simplest ways into paper-filtered pour-over coffee, and it's what our December 2019 video shows.

<LiteYouTube videoId="9GTiZ9IVkGA" title="Making Coffee with the OXO Brew Pour Over!" />

The video runs through a whole brew without narration: grinding the beans, rinsing the paper filter in the cone, adding the grounds, setting the tank on top, filling it from a kettle and covering it while it drains. As its description says, I bought the brewer on Amazon and had been using it for a week when I made it.

## Which OXO brewer this is

OXO now sells it as the **Pour-Over Coffee Maker with Water Tank** (item 11180100), part of its Brew line; Amazon lists it as the OXO Brew Single Serve Pour-Over Coffee Maker with Water Tank. It comes in three pieces, as in our video: the cone, the clear water tank and a lid. As published by the maker:

- **Capacity:** markings on the tank show how much water to add, up to 12 oz.
- **The tank:** its hole pattern spreads the hot water evenly over the grounds at a set rate.
- **The lid:** it holds in heat while the coffee brews, then works as a drip tray.
- **Filters:** #2 cone filters. Ten OXO unbleached #2 filters come in the box.
- **Materials and care:** polypropylene and Tritan, BPA-free, and every part is dishwasher safe.

OXO listed it at $19.99 on its own store when checked October 18, 2026.

**A correction:** an earlier version of this page said the OXO takes size-4 cone filters. OXO's page and instructions call for #2.

## OXO's recipe

OXO's instruction card gives the coffee for each tank marking, in grams and in tablespoons. The ratios are calculated, taking 1 ml of water as 1 g:

| Water (tank marking) | Coffee | OXO's spoon measure | Ratio (calculated) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 oz (180 ml) | 10 g | 2 tbsp | 1:18 |
| 8 oz (240 ml) | 13 g | 2 tbsp + 2 tsp | about 1:18.5 |
| 10 oz (300 ml) | 16 g | 3 tbsp + ½ tsp | about 1:18.8 |
| 12 oz (360 ml) | 20 g | 4 tbsp | 1:18 |

Its steps, in order:

1. Place a #2 filter in the dripper and add medium-ground coffee.
2. Place the water tank on top of the dripper.
3. Pour boiling water into the tank up to the measurement marking you want.
4. Cover with the lid and wait for all the water to drain, about 2½ to 3 minutes.

There's no separate bloom: the tank lets the water down gradually from the start. OXO's steps don't mention rinsing the filter, though our video rinses it with hot water first. Jonathan Gagné, an astrophysicist who studies brewing, steeped V60 papers in hot water and tasted it: the unbleached one had the strongest taste, which reminded him of wet cardboard, but after he pre-washed the filters he could barely notice any difference. He notes that other brands of filter may differ.

A full 12 oz tank makes about 11 US fl oz (320 ml) in the cup. That's calculated: 360 g of water, less about 40 g held back by 20 g of grounds at roughly 2 g per gram, the figure our Brew Guide uses.

## Weighing it out in the Brew Guide

OXO's amounts are a little lighter than our Brew Guide's starting strength for a pour-over cone, 1:16. If the cup tastes weak rather than sour or bitter, try more coffee for the same water: about 22½ g for the 12 oz marking would be 1:16 (calculated). OXO says boiling water; the Brew Guide's temperatures are for pouring by hand into a V60, so with the OXO, follow OXO.

## The grind

OXO asks for medium-ground coffee. Our [grind size guide](/blog/coffee-grind-size-guide-what-grind-for-every-brew-method/) shows what that looks like. From there, change the grind one step at a time by taste: finer if the cup is sour or thin, coarser if it's bitter or harsh. James Hoffmann explains that a burr grinder cuts beans into evenly sized pieces and can be set to a size, while a blade grinder smashes them into a mix of powder and large pieces.

If your grinder is OXO's own Brew Conical Burr grinder, the Grind Calibrator shows the range OXO publishes for pour-over: settings 6 to 10 on its 1–15 dial, the same range OXO gives for a drip machine.

## How it compares

- **Hario V60.** A cone with spiral ribs, which Hario says support the filter and keep air flowing, and one large hole. Where and how fast you pour is up to you, which gives more control and more to learn. Our [V60 guide](/blog/ultimate-pour-over-coffee-guide-and-best-v60-technique/) has a full recipe.
- **A flat-bottom dripper such as the Kalita Wave.** Its own flat filters and a flat bed; see our [flat-bottom vs cone guide](/blog/flat-bottom-vs-cone-pour-over/).
- **Clever Dripper.** Takes the pour out of it another way: the coffee steeps before it drains. See our [Clever Dripper guide](/blog/the-clever-dripper-better-coffee-than-pour-over/).

## Who it suits

Going by OXO's specifications, it suits one person brewing a cup at a time, up to 12 oz, who wants paper-filtered coffee without learning to pour. It needs no gooseneck kettle, since the tank does the pouring, and it takes #2 cone filters. It's less suited to brewing for several people at once, or to anyone who wants to adjust the pour itself.
