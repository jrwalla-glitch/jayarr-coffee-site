Starbucks sells Nitro Cold Brew on tap in its cafés, and since 2020 in cans made with PepsiCo. In September 2020 we bought an eight-pack of the black, unsweetened cans online, at about 3 dollars a can, to see whether the nitrogen really makes canned cold brew smoother, and whether that's worth the price. The tasting and verdict below are from that video. The product details were checked on October 18, 2026.

<LiteYouTube videoId="Qo-7nCDjjUM" title="Starbucks Canned Nitro Cold Brew REVIEW! (Worth 3 dollars a can?)" />

## How the can works

Starbucks and PepsiCo launched the cans on February 26, 2020, in three flavors: Black, Vanilla Sweet Cream and Dark Caramel. Their announcement says the cold brew "is infused with nitrogen, and the moment the can is opened a rush of microbubbles delivers a delicious, velvety smooth taste."

The instructions we read off the back of the can were simple: gently tilt the can once, pop it open and pour into a cold glass. Poured slowly, it didn't fizz like a soda; there were just a few bubbles at the top of the glass.

For its café Nitro Cold Brew, Starbucks says the nitrogen creates "a sweet flavor without sugar and cascading, velvety crema." That's the company's description of its drink, not something we measured. An earlier version of this page explained the effect with a widget inside the can and claims about how nitrogen fools the palate and slows oxidation; we couldn't find a Starbucks or PepsiCo source for any of them, so they're gone.

## What we found (2020)

**Black.** The aroma was the best part: rich and strong. It went down smooth, but it tasted a little bitter, with a slightly burnt edge, like a strong dark coffee. Out of the can it tasted a little different, which seemed to be about texture. I'm not a big fan of black coffee unless it's a really premium one, so I wouldn't drink this black.

**With a splash of half-and-half.** "That's actually really amazing." The half-and-half smoothed it out and took away most of the bitter aftertaste, and this is the version I'd happily drink all day.

**The price.** At about 3 dollars a can, bought in bulk, it felt a little pricey, especially for a can I'd want to add half-and-half to, which takes away some of the grab-and-go convenience.

**Verdict.** Overall it's not bad: the nitro does make it smoother, and I was satisfied with it. If you're a coffee lover, give it a shot. The video gives no score out of 10.

An earlier version of this page summed the video up as "too bitter for most casual drinkers" and listed reasons to skip it. The video's own verdict was kinder: bitter black for my taste, very good with half-and-half, and worth a try. The text now follows the video.

## What's in the can now

PepsiCo still lists **Starbucks Nitro Cold Brew Black** on PepsiCo Partners, its site for business customers, in 9.6 oz cans, 12 to a case, when checked October 18, 2026. As published there:

- **Description:** a "custom blend of beans," cold brewed in small batches and "slow-steeped in cool water for 20 hours, without touching heat."
- **Ingredients:** cold brewed Starbucks coffee (water, coffee) and nitrogen.
- **Per can:** 10 calories, 0 g of fat, 2 g of carbohydrate and 1 g of protein, and not a significant source of sugars or added sugars. That's the same 10 calories the can showed in our 2020 video.

We couldn't check whether the Vanilla Sweet Cream and Dark Caramel cans from the 2020 launch are still sold. Starbucks' other canned coffee, Cold Brew in 11 oz cans with cream flavors, is a separate line: its March 2024 announcement doesn't describe it as nitro.

At launch, the suggested retail prices listed in the February 2020 announcement were 3.99 dollars a can at convenience stores and 3.49 dollars in grocery stores. The "3 dollars a can" in the title is what we paid for our eight-pack online in 2020, not a current price.

## How much caffeine is in it?

PepsiCo Product Facts lists 155 mg of caffeine for one 9.6 fl oz (284 ml) can of Starbucks Nitro Black Unsweet, checked October 18, 2026. Recipes and labels change, so check the can you buy.

An earlier version of this page gave "about 215 mg per can." That matches Starbucks' approximate figure for a tall (12 fl oz) Nitro Cold Brew in its cafés; Starbucks lists a grande (16 fl oz) at about 280 mg. Both are bigger drinks than the 9.6 fl oz can, so neither is the can's figure. For scale, the can's 155 mg is about two to two and a half single espresso shots at the Caffeine Calculator's 60 to 75 mg a shot (calculated). Our [caffeine guide](/blog/caffeine-in-coffee-guide/) has the FDA's guidance on daily amounts.
