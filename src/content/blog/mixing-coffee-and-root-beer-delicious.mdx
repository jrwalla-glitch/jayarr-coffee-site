I was skeptical. Coffee meets candy-sweet soda? Then I tried it. The root beer's sweetness tempers the coffee's bitterness, the coffee keeps the soda from tasting saccharine, and the fizz makes the whole thing lighter.

Root beer, in Merriam-Webster's definition, is a sweetened, usually carbonated brown drink flavored with extracts of roots such as sarsaparilla or sassafras and herbs such as wintergreen, or with artificial flavorings. Our 2020 video pairs it with cold brew and cream of coconut. The amounts below are the ones on the video's screen; the video predates this revision.

## The Coconut Root Beer Refresher

**For one drink:**

- **Cold brew:** 150 ml (about 5 US fl oz), chilled, at drinking strength. If yours is concentrate, mix it one to one with cold water first, as Jessica Easto's cold brew recipe and our [cold brew concentrate guide](/blog/cold-brew-concentrate-guide/) do.
- **Root beer:** 50 ml (about 1¾ fl oz), chilled.
- **Cream of coconut:** 50 ml (about 1¾ fl oz). This is the sweetened kind used in cocktails, not unsweetened coconut cream: Coco López describes its original as coconut cream blended with cane sugar.
- **Crushed ice.**
- **Makes:** about 250 ml (8½ fl oz) before the ice, calculated from the amounts above.
- **Time:** about 3 minutes, with the cold brew already made.

**Method:**

1. Pour 150 ml of chilled cold brew into a glass.
2. Add 50 ml of root beer and 50 ml of cream of coconut.
3. Stir until the cream of coconut is mixed in.
4. Add crushed ice and serve.

The video's description adds one more step before you start: put the glass in the freezer for an hour or so. For a plainer drink, leave out the cream of coconut and top the cold brew with root beer to taste.

## Hot or cold coffee?

Our advice: chill the coffee for any drink where the soda goes straight onto it, as here, and save a hot shot for floats, where it lands on the ice cream first. Carbon dioxide dissolves less in warmer water. Stir a 1 fl oz shot at about 158°F&nbsp;(70°C) into 6 fl oz of soda from the fridge at 39°F&nbsp;(4°C), and the mix lands near 56°F&nbsp;(13°C), counting both as water and leaving out the glass and the ice. Using NIST's constants for pure water, the equilibrium dissolved CO₂ concentration at about 56°F&nbsp;(13.4°C) is roughly 25% lower than at 39°F&nbsp;(4°C) at the same CO₂ partial pressure. This simplified calculation illustrates the temperature effect; it does not measure how much fizz the drink loses while you make it. A hot shot costs fizz; cold coffee keeps more of it.

## Which root beer?

The video doesn't name its root beer, and any will do. Check the label for caffeine and sugar, because they vary. Barq's Root Beer, for example, lists 22 mg of caffeine and 44 g of sugars per 12 fl oz can, while Barq's Zero Sugar is caffeine free (Coca-Cola's Barq's page, checked October 18, 2026). The 50 ml in this drink is about a seventh of a can: about 3 mg of caffeine and 6 g of sugar from regular Barq's (calculated).

Virgil's, made by Reed's Inc., says its root beer uses a blend of 15 roots and spices. You can find <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Virgil%27s+root+beer&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Virgil's root beer on Amazon</a> if it isn't stocked near you.

## A root beer float version

For a float, follow our [coffee and cola ice cream float](/blog/delicious-coffee-and-cola-ice-cream-float/) with root beer in place of the cola: a scoop of ice cream in the glass, a shot of espresso poured over it, then root beer poured slowly down the side. That's the one place on these pages where hot coffee goes in, because the ice cream takes the heat first.
