Cream soda is, in Merriam-Webster's definition, a carbonated soft drink flavored with vanilla, so this is, in effect, a fizzy vanilla iced coffee. Our 2020 video calls it the Charged Up Cream Soda. The amounts below are the ones on the video's screen; the video predates this revision.

## The Charged Up Cream Soda

**For one drink:**

- **Coffee ice cubes:** a handful. Freeze cold coffee in an ice cube tray ahead of time. As they melt they add coffee, not water.
- **Ice-cold coffee:** 100 ml (about 3½ US fl oz). The video's description uses cold brew. If yours is concentrate, mix it one to one with cold water first, as Jessica Easto's recipe and our [cold brew concentrate guide](/blog/cold-brew-concentrate-guide/) do. Hot-brewed coffee chilled in the fridge works too, or brew it straight onto ice as in our [Japanese iced coffee guide](/blog/japanese-iced-coffee-vs-cold-brew/).
- **Ice-cold cream soda:** 100 ml (about 3½ fl oz).
- **Makes:** 200 ml (about 6¾ fl oz) plus the ice, calculated from the amounts above.
- **Time:** about 2 minutes, with the coffee and the ice cubes already made.

**Method:**

1. Put the coffee ice cubes in a glass.
2. Pour in 100 ml of ice-cold coffee.
3. Slowly pour 100 ml of ice-cold cream soda down the side of the glass, so the foam doesn't overflow.

The video's description also chills the glass first: put it in the freezer for an hour or so. Equal parts coffee and soda is the video's mix; use more soda for a sweeter drink, or more coffee for a stronger one.

## Hot or cold coffee?

Our advice: chill the coffee for any drink where the soda goes straight onto it, as here, and save a hot shot for floats, where it lands on the ice cream first. Carbon dioxide dissolves less in warmer water. Stir a 1 fl oz shot at about 158°F&nbsp;(70°C) into 6 fl oz of soda from the fridge at 39°F&nbsp;(4°C), and the mix lands near 56°F&nbsp;(13°C), counting both as water and leaving out the glass and the ice. Using NIST's constants for pure water, the equilibrium dissolved CO₂ concentration at about 56°F&nbsp;(13.4°C) is roughly 25% lower than at 39°F&nbsp;(4°C) at the same CO₂ partial pressure. This simplified calculation illustrates the temperature effect; it does not measure how much fizz the drink loses while you make it. A hot shot costs fizz; cold coffee keeps more of it.

## How much caffeine?

The caffeine is the coffee's, plus any that the cream soda's label lists. Cold brew's caffeine varies a lot with the beans and the recipe, so the calculator gives a range, and the coffee ice cubes add more as they melt.
