Coffee is a seasonal crop that most of us buy like a pantry staple. Knowing when each origin is picked, and roughly when it lands, helps you read a "new crop" label and spot a coffee that has been sitting in a warehouse for a long time.

## Three dates that shape your coffee

- **The harvest, or crop year.** When the cherries were picked. The International Coffee Organization (ICO) counts producing countries' crop years from April 1, July 1 or October 1, depending on when their harvests fall. A "2025/26" Ethiopian coffee, for example, was picked from about October 2025 into early 2026.
- **The arrival.** When the green coffee reached an importer in the country where it will be roasted. Processing, drying, milling and shipping take months.
- **The roast date,** the one printed on retail bags. Our Freshness tool treats about 7 to 21 days after roasting as the usual best window and about a month as the end of the good window for whole beans: typical windows rather than a forecast for your bag.

A fresh roast date tells you nothing about the green coffee's age. A roaster can roast an old crop today and print today's date on the bag. The best bags are fresh at all three stages.

## How long green coffee stays fresh

Green coffee ages slowly, but it does age. The Specialty Coffee Association's "Coffee Decoded" series describes how it goes: some coffees lose their spark in a few months, while others keep their character for 14 or 16 months. As they age, flavors mute, aromas become less lively, and a washed coffee's color fades from gray-green toward pale beige. Cool, dry storage slows it, sealed plastic or mylar bags help, and deep-freezing nearly stops it.

By the time the next harvest arrives, the trade calls the previous year's coffee "past crop." That's why timing matters when you buy. A "new arrival" Kenyan coffee in April fits the calendar: Kenya's main crop is picked October to December, and the importer Café Imports lists typical Kenyan arrivals from March to May. A "new arrival" Kenyan in September deserves a question about which crop it came from.

## Origin by origin

Most coffee grows between the tropics. Broadly, origins north of the equator pick from about October to March, and those south of it from about April to September, with exceptions such as Sumatra and Tanzania. Countries near the equator, like Colombia and Kenya, can have two crops a year. These are the calendars behind the chart, with the arrival windows Café Imports publishes.

### Africa

- **Ethiopia:** October to January in Sidama and Yirgacheffe, November to January in Limu and Jima, and February to April in Ghimbi and Lekempti in the west, says Hoffmann. Café Imports lists April to June arrivals.
- **Kenya:** Hoffmann gives every region a main crop from October to December and a smaller fly crop from June to August. Café Imports' calendar is wider: August to January for the main crop and April to July for the early crop, with arrivals from March to May. Our [Kenyan coffee guide](/blog/kenyan-coffee-flavor-notes-and-best-coffees/) covers what that crop tastes like.
- **Rwanda:** one crop a year. NAEB, Rwanda's agricultural export board, says picking starts in late February on the shores of Lake Kivu and early March elsewhere, and runs until August in some northern areas. Hoffmann gives March to June. Café Imports lists September to December arrivals. See our [Rwandan coffee guide](/blog/rwandan-coffee-a-story-of-rebuilding/).
- **Burundi:** April to July (Hoffmann), arriving October to December.
- **Tanzania:** July to December in the northern regions around Kilimanjaro and Arusha, June to October in the southern highlands (Hoffmann). Café Imports lists January to April arrivals.

### Central America and Mexico

These harvests cluster in the northern dry season, so the region's new crops tend to land together in late spring and summer.

- **Guatemala:** December to March in most regions, January to April in Huehuetenango and January to March in Antigua (Hoffmann). Café Imports lists June arrivals. See our [Guatemalan coffee guide](/blog/guatemala-coffee-review-regional-differences-and-best-coffees/).
- **Costa Rica:** November to March in Tarrazú, the Central Valley and Tres Ríos, but from as early as July or August in Turrialba, Brunca, Orosi and Guanacaste (Hoffmann); arrivals May to July. Our [Costa Rican coffee guide](/blog/what-does-costa-rican-coffee-taste-like/) has the regional table.
- **Honduras:** from November in Copán and Opalaca and from December elsewhere, finishing between February and April (Hoffmann); arrivals May to July. See our [Honduras coffee guide](/blog/honduras-coffee-guide-marcala-comayagua/).
- **El Salvador:** October to March across its mountain ranges (Hoffmann); arrivals May to July. See our [El Salvador coffee guide](/blog/el-salvador-coffee-guide-pacamara/).
- **Nicaragua:** December to March (Hoffmann); arrivals April to July.
- **Panama:** December to March in Boquete, Volcán-Candela and Renacimiento (Hoffmann); arrivals April to July. See our [Panama coffee guide](/blog/panama-coffee-guide-geisha-boquete/).
- **Mexico:** November to March in Chiapas, December to March in Oaxaca and Veracruz (Hoffmann); arrivals May to June.

### South America

- **Brazil:** May to September in every region Hoffmann lists. In 2026 the ICO reported that a survey by the consultancy Safras & Mercado found the harvest 97 percent complete by August 26. Café Imports lists October to January arrivals. See our [Brazilian coffee guide](/blog/is-brazilian-coffee-any-good/).
- **Colombia:** two crops, and which is "main" depends on the region. Studies at six of Cenicafé's research stations in the central coffee zone (Cenicafé is the research center of Colombia's coffee growers' federation) found the main harvest starting in August, building from September and falling off sharply in December, with the second crop, the *traviesa* or *mitaca*, usually picked from March to May. Hoffmann's regional tables put the main crop at September to December in Huila, Caldas, Antioquia and Risaralda, but March to June in Cauca, Tolima and Cundinamarca. Café Imports lists Colombian arrivals as year-round. See our [Colombian coffee guide](/blog/single-origin-colombian-coffee-what-flavor-notes-to-expect/).
- **Peru:** March to September in Cajamarca, Junín, Cusco and San Martín (Hoffmann). Café Imports lists January to June arrivals. See our [Peruvian coffee guide](/blog/peru-coffee-guide-chanchamayo/).

### Asia and Oceania

- **Indonesia:** each island has its own season. Hoffmann gives September to December for Sumatra, July to September for Java and May to November for Sulawesi. Café Imports lists Sumatran arrivals as year-round, Java's in November and December and Sulawesi's from September to December. See our [Sumatra coffee reviews](/blog/7-sumatra-coffees-reviewed-best-and-worst/).
- **India:** October to February across the regions Hoffmann lists; Café Imports lists arrivals from March to December. See our [Indian coffee guide](/blog/what-is-coffee-from-india-like/).
- **Yemen:** October to December (Hoffmann). Café Imports says its arrivals vary. See our [Yemen coffee guide](/blog/yemen-coffee-guide-mocha-origin/).
- **Papua New Guinea:** April to September in the highlands (Hoffmann); arrivals October to November.
- **Hawaii:** August to January in Kona and the Big Island's other districts, with other islands from September or October (Hoffmann). Café Imports lists US arrivals from December to March. See our [Kaʻū coffee guide](/blog/ka-u-hawaii-specialty-coffee-region/).

## What's newly arriving, season by season

Reading across the arrival windows above (Café Imports' typical US arrivals):

- **January to March:** Brazil's last arrivals, then Kenya's main crop from March. Tanzania, Peru and Hawaii are landing, and Colombia and Sumatra arrive year-round.
- **April to June:** Ethiopia, the rest of Kenya's main crop and most of Central America and Mexico. Peru is still landing.
- **July to September:** the tail of the Central American arrivals, then Rwanda and Sulawesi from September. India's window runs from March to December.
- **October to December:** Brazil's new crop, Burundi, Rwanda, Papua New Guinea, Java and Sulawesi, with Hawaii from December.

There's almost always something newly arrived. Rotating origins with the seasons is one way to keep the green coffee in your cup recent.

## Buying with the seasons

- **Ask about the harvest and arrival.** A good specialty roaster knows when its green coffee was picked and when it landed.
- **Watch for "new crop" and "fresh arrival."** Roasters and importers announce new crops because freshness sells. Check the claim against the calendar above.
- **Question the same single origin all year.** A roaster offering the same Kenyan lot in March and September may be roasting from the same green coffee for months. Colombia, with its two regional crops and year-round arrivals, is the usual exception.
- **Freeze what you love.** Roasted coffee keeps far longer in the freezer: our Freshness tool pauses the clock for up to about a year of freezing. Our [guide to freezing coffee beans](/blog/how-to-freeze-coffee-beans/) covers the method.

Coffee is closer to fruit than to rice: it has seasons. Asking "what's newly arrived?" is a simple way to put fresher coffee in your cup.
