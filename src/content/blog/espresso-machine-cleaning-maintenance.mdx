Every shot leaves coffee oils and fine grounds in the basket, the group and the shower screen, and hard water leaves scale inside. Breville's manuals warn that scale reduces "the brewing flow, brewing temperature, power of the machine, and taste of the espresso." Gaggia's says regular cleaning keeps the coffee flow constant and the flavor right.

What doesn't carry over from one machine to the next is the schedule and the chemistry. Rancilio has you backflush the Silvia every day; Breville's Bambino has no backflush step at all; and Gaggia allows only its own descaler. So this guide sets out the habits the manuals share, then the steps that differ, with the makers' own instructions as examples. Your manual is the authority for your machine.

## Daily care the manuals share

- **Wipe and purge the steam wand after milk.** Breville says the Bambino's wand "should always be cleaned after texturing milk", with a damp cloth. Gaggia's E24 manual says to wipe it after each frothing and open the steam knob for a second or two to clear the nozzle. Rancilio's Silvia manual adds a daily soak of the wand in a milk cleaning solution.
- **Rinse the basket and portafilter.** Breville says to rinse them under hot water directly after use, to remove the coffee oils, and not to put the portafilter in the dishwasher. Gaggia says to take the filter out of the filter holder every day and wash it in warm water.
- **Clean the group the way your manual says.** Breville says to wipe the group head and shower screen with a damp cloth, and now and then run hot water through the basket and portafilter with no coffee in them. Gaggia says to brush grounds off the screen regularly. Rancilio says to brush the seal under the group head, but: "Do not brush the shower!"
- **Empty the drip tray.** Breville says after each use, or when the drip tray indicator rises; Gaggia says to wash the tray and grill with water and no abrasive tools.
- **Use fresh water.** Breville's Barista Express manual says to replace the water daily, and Gaggia says to use fresh water every time you use the machine.

## Backflushing: only if your manual calls for it

A backflush locks a blind filter, or a cleaning disc, into the group: a basket with no holes. The water and cleaner can't pass through it, so the machine releases them into the drip tray instead; Gaggia's manual describes the detergent water flowing into the drip tray through the drain pipe, and Gaggia credits the E24's three-way solenoid valve with releasing the pressure after each shot. Do it only if your manual includes it, with the cleaner it names. If your manual has no backflush step, don't add one.

How it differs, machine by machine:

- **Gaggia Classic Pro E24:** monthly, with a quarter of a Gaggia Coffee Oil Remover tablet in the blind filter (both sold separately). Press the coffee button, wait 5 seconds, press it again to stop the pump, and wait about 30 seconds while the water drains; repeat at least 10 times, until clean water comes out. Then wash the filter holder and blind filter in hot water and rinse the same way, at least 10 more times, without a tablet.
- **Rancilio Silvia:** daily, with the blind filter disk and an espresso machine detergent. Switch the coffee switch on for 5 seconds and off for 10, and repeat until the water runs clear into the drip tray.
- **Ascaso Steel Duo PID:** weekly, with the blind filter, pressing the coffee switch for 10 seconds twice. It also has a cleaning mode, CLn.
- **Breville Barista Express:** a cleaning cycle that Breville calls back-flushing, run when the CLEAN / DESCALE light flashes. Put the cleaning disc and one cleaning tablet in the 1 CUP basket; the cycle takes about 5 minutes, and you check afterward that the tablet has dissolved.
- **Breville Bambino Plus:** a flushing cycle, due after 200 extractions, with the cleaning disc and one cleaning tablet.
- **Breville Bambino:** no separate backflush. It has a group head rinse from the 2 Cup button, and a combined clean and descale cycle (below).
- **Super-automatics:** their own programs. JURA's E8 asks for its cleaning program after 180 preparations or 80 switch-on rinses, with a JURA cleaning tablet in the filler funnel, for about 20 minutes. The Philips 3200 LatteGo's brew group comes out so you can rinse it under the tap.

## Descaling: the maker's schedule and the maker's descaler

There's no universal descaling interval. Some makers give a fixed one, some count cups, and some build a signal into the machine. Most also name the descaler to use.

- **Gaggia Classic Pro E24:** every 2 months, with Gaggia's descaling product only: "Never use vinegar or other descaling agents." Gaggia's product page adds "whenever you notice a reduction in the water flow," and warns against descalers based on mineral acids.
- **De'Longhi Stilosa EC260BK:** about every 200 coffees, with De'Longhi descaler.
- **De'Longhi Dedica Duo EC890M:** when the B5 indicator lights; the cycle takes about 30 minutes.
- **Breville Bambino:** one combined clean and descale cycle when the 2 Cup button flashes continuously. Breville gives no fixed interval and advises against ignoring the warning.
- **Breville Bambino Plus:** a separate descaling cycle, when the 1 CUP+STEAM and 2 CUP buttons flash alternately; no fixed interval.
- **Breville Barista Express:** when the CLEAN / DESCALE light is on solid (flashing means the cleaning cycle), with one Breville descaling powder sachet in 34 fl oz (1 L) of water.
- **JURA E8 (NAC):** when the machine asks, with 3 JURA descaling tablets in 500 ml of water, for about 40 minutes. JURA says the machine doesn't ask for descaling while a CLEARYL Smart+ filter is in use.
- **Philips 3200 LatteGo:** guided descaling. Philips says its AquaClean filter allows up to 5,000 cups without descaling if you change the filter whenever the machine prompts; its footnote says actual counts vary.
- **Profitec GO 2.0:** a menu-guided program, with Profitec's original descaler only.
- **Profitec Pro 400:** descaling may be needed as prevention, but Profitec says a machine that is already calcified may only be descaled by a specialized dealer.

An earlier version of this guide gave a citric acid recipe and a descaling schedule by water hardness. Both are gone: Gaggia rules out any descaler but its own, and the makers above each set their own schedule. Use the product, the amounts and the timing in your manual.

## Water: what your manual asks for

Makers disagree about water, so this is another place where the manual decides.

- **Breville** says, in the Bambino's manual, not to use "highly filtered, de-mineralized or distilled water," because it may affect the taste and how the machine is designed to work; use cold tap water. The Barista Express manual recommends cold, filtered water, fits a water filter in the tank, and says to replace it every two months, or more often in hard water, to reduce the need to descale. It says to take the filter out if you use filtered or bottled water.
- **Gaggia** recommends "purified or bottled water" for the best-tasting coffee in its E24 manual, and still recommends periodic descaling.
- **Rancilio** says the Silvia's water should have a carbonate hardness of about 5&nbsp;°dKH (89&nbsp;ppm), and recommends a suitable filtering system if yours doesn't match; your water supplier, or a hardness test, will tell you.
- **Super-automatics** often have their own filters: Philips's AquaClean for the 3200, and JURA's CLEARYL Smart+ for the E8.

Our [water guide](/blog/why-water-matters-more-than-your-coffee-beans/) and [water recipe comparison](/blog/coffee-water-recipes-compared/) are about brewing water and taste. For the water that goes into your machine, your manual's specification comes first.

## Seals, screens and repairs

Gaskets and shower screens wear with use. When to replace them, and whether to do it yourself, is in your manual or a question for an authorized service center. Gaggia's US manual, for instance, says any servicing beyond cleaning and user maintenance should be done by an authorized service center, and that there are no user-serviceable parts inside.
