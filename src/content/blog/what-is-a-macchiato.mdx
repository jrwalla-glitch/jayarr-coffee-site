*Macchiato* means "marked" or "stained." James Hoffmann explains where the name comes from: an Italian barista lines up several espressos on the bar, and when a customer wants just a drop of milk, the barista adds a small dollop of foam to mark which cup is theirs. A drop of plain milk would vanish under the crema.

That's the macchiato of the Italian bar. The same word now names two other drinks as well, so the surest way to get the one you want is to know all three.

## Three drinks with one name

Each row below is one published recipe or menu listing, not a universal definition.

| Drink, by whose recipe | Espresso | Milk | Cup |
|---|---|---|---|
| Espresso macchiato, illy | A single, 20–25 ml | Foam from about 28 ml of whole milk, poured on | Small, filled nearly to the brim |
| Espresso macchiato, Breville | One shot | Two teaspoons of microfoam | Small glass or demitasse, 2–3 fl oz |
| Espresso Macchiato, Starbucks | Two shots in the default Doppio | A dollop of steamed milk and foam | Doppio, listed at 1.6 fl oz |
| Caffè Macchiato, Peet's | A ristretto: single, double or triple | A dash of steamed milk and foam | Not listed |
| Latte macchiato, La Cimbali | One espresso, poured in last | 130 ml of hot frothed milk, about 1.5 cm of foam | Tall glass, or a 160 ml cappuccino cup |
| Macchiato, Dunkin' | Two shots, on top | "Creamy milk" | Not listed |
| Caramel Macchiato, Starbucks | Two shots in a Grande | Steamed 2% milk, three pumps of vanilla syrup, caramel drizzle | Grande, 16 fl oz |

Read down the milk column and the drinks fall into two families. In an espresso macchiato the coffee comes first and the milk is a mark on top. In a latte macchiato, Dunkin's Macchiato and Starbucks' Caramel Macchiato, the milk comes first and the espresso is the mark.

## The espresso macchiato

This is the small one, and the one Hoffmann describes. The illy recipe is a single espresso of 20–25 ml (about ¾ US fl oz) with the foam from about 28 ml of whole milk poured on top, filling the small cup almost to the brim. Breville's version uses less: two teaspoons of microfoam on one shot. Either way it's mostly espresso, softened by a little milk.

The chains keep the idea and change the size. Starbucks' Espresso Macchiato is espresso marked with a dollop of steamed milk and foam, in Solo, Doppio, Triple and Quad sizes; the default Doppio has two shots and is listed at 1.6 fl oz (about 47 ml, calculated). Peet's Caffè Macchiato starts from a ristretto, a short shot, marked with a dash of steamed milk and foam.

Specialty cafés don't all agree either. Hoffmann notes that many quality-focused coffee shops have turned the macchiato into an espresso topped up with foamed milk, often because customers ask for a longer, weaker, sweeter drink.

## The latte macchiato

A latte macchiato turns the drink around: the milk is stained by the coffee. In Cimbali's recipe you heat and froth the milk, pour 130 ml of it into the glass, then pour one espresso in. About 1.5 cm of foam sits on top.

Dunkin's Macchiato is built the same way round: creamy milk topped with two shots of espresso.

## The Caramel Macchiato

Starbucks created the Caramel Macchiato as a limited-time drink for its 25th anniversary in 1996, and by the company's account it went on to start a new beverage category. Starbucks describes it as freshly steamed milk with vanilla syrup, marked with espresso and topped with a caramel drizzle.

The Grande is 16 fl oz, with two shots, three pumps of vanilla syrup and 2% milk by default. Starbucks lists it at 250 calories and 33 g of sugar. Hoffmann calls it an entirely different drink from the Italian macchiato, much closer to a caffè latte marked with caramel. It has caused some confusion, he writes, especially in North America, which is why many coffee shops now call the small drink a "traditional macchiato."

## How to make an espresso macchiato at home

You need an espresso machine with a steam wand. A macchiato starts from espresso, so a moka pot's coffee with foam on top is a stand-in, not the same drink. Here is illy's recipe, with Breville's milk temperature.

1. **Pull a single espresso** of 20–25 ml into a small cup. Our [dial-in guide](/blog/how-to-dial-in-espresso/) covers getting the shot right.
2. **Foam a little whole milk** with the steam wand. illy uses about 28 ml. Breville, writing for its home machines, steams 100 ml in the jug, heats it to 140–149°F&nbsp;(60–65°C) and uses just two teaspoons of the foam.
3. **Pour or spoon the foam onto the espresso.** illy fills the cup almost to the brim; Breville's two teaspoons just mark the top.

Our [milk steaming guide](/blog/how-to-steam-milk-for-lattes/) explains how to get fine foam and how hot to go.

## How to make a latte macchiato at home

1. **Steam and froth the milk,** enough for about 130 ml once frothed, Cimbali's amount.
2. **Pour the milk into a tall glass.**
3. **Pull one espresso and pour it into the milk.**

## How to order the one you want

At a specialty café, ask for a "traditional macchiato" if you want the small drink, since some shops now make theirs bigger and milkier. Or just ask how they make it.

At Starbucks, order an Espresso Macchiato for the small drink and a Caramel Macchiato for the sweet milk drink. At Peet's, the Caffè Macchiato is a ristretto marked with milk and foam. At Dunkin', a Macchiato is milk topped with espresso, closer to a latte macchiato.

If you're still finding your way around the menu, our [coffee shop ordering guide](/blog/how-to-order-at-coffee-shop/) covers the other drinks, including the [cortado](/blog/what-is-a-cortado/) and the [flat white](/blog/what-is-a-flat-white-latte-vs-flat-white-comparison/).

## How much caffeine is in a macchiato?

The caffeine comes from the espresso, so count the shots. Our Caffeine Calculator puts a single shot at about 60–75 mg. Starbucks lists both its Doppio Espresso Macchiato and its Grande Caramel Macchiato at about 150 mg, since each has two shots. The big drink has a lot more milk and sugar, not more coffee.
