## Before you start: the coffee and the milk

**Espresso.** The salted mocha caramel latte and the caramel brûlée latte use 2 shots, a double, which our Brew Guide makes as 36 g in the cup from 18 g of coffee.

<ToolCallout tool="brew" preset={{ method: 'espresso', dose: 18 }} cta="Open the Brew Guide for an 18 g double" context="Opens the Brew Guide's espresso at 18 g of coffee: 36 g in the cup in 25–30 seconds, at 199°F (93°C) for a medium roast, with a timer to brew along." />

**Strong brewed coffee.** The other recipes call for hot, strongly brewed coffee. Our Brew Guide's drip machine, at its Stronger setting, makes an 8 oz cup from 17.5 g of coffee and 272 g of water, about 1:15.5.

<ToolCallout tool="brew" preset={{ method: 'drip-machine', cup: 236.6, strength: 'stronger' }} cta="Open the Brew Guide for a strong 8 oz cup" context="Opens the Brew Guide's drip machine on its Stronger setting for an 8 oz cup: 17.5 g of coffee to 272 g of water (1:15.5), with a medium grind of about 600–900 µm. For a bigger batch, such as the toffee coffee's 5 cups, choose Custom and enter 40 fl oz." />

No espresso machine? A [moka pot](/blog/moka-pot-brewing-guide/) or an [AeroPress](/blog/why-and-how-you-should-use-an-aeropress/) makes a small, strong coffee to use in place of the shots. Neither is espresso, so the drinks will taste a little different.

**How hot is the milk?** If you have a thermometer, Breville's Bambino manual has milk ready at 140–149°F&nbsp;(60–65°C). James Hoffmann writes that milk begins to deteriorate in flavor and texture above 154°F&nbsp;(68°C), and that he finds milk heated to 140°F&nbsp;(60°C) better in sweetness and texture than very hot milk. Scott Rao's café target is 150–160°F&nbsp;(66–71°C), and the top of that is past Hoffmann's limit. In a saucepan, take the milk off before it simmers. Our [milk steaming guide](/blog/how-to-steam-milk-for-lattes/) covers a steam wand.

## 1. Salted mocha caramel latte

Our own recipe, and the first in the video: a mocha with caramel, finished with a little salt.

- **Milk:** 1 cup (8 US fl oz, about 240 ml). The notes allow a milk substitute.
- **Coffee:** 2 shots of espresso, or 1 cup of strong brewed coffee.
- **Caramel syrup:** 2 tablespoons (30 ml).
- **Brown sugar:** 2 tablespoons (about 28 g packed). The notes allow any sweetener: white sugar, honey or brown sugar.
- **Cocoa powder:** 1 tablespoon (about 5 g).
- **To top:** whipped cream, a drizzle of caramel sauce and a pinch of sea salt. The video's card says ⅛ tablespoon of salt (about ⅜ teaspoon), but the notes say just a pinch, so start with a pinch.
- **Makes:** about 11 US fl oz (325 ml) with espresso, or about 18 fl oz (525 ml) with a cup of brewed coffee, before the foam and toppings (calculated from the volumes above).
- **Time:** about 10 minutes.

1. Heat the milk in a small saucepan over medium heat until it steams, without letting it boil.
2. Froth the hot milk with a frother, a French press or a whisk.
3. Pull 2 shots of espresso into a mug, or pour in 1 cup of strong coffee.
4. Stir in the caramel syrup, brown sugar and cocoa powder until no dry powder is left.
5. Pour in the frothed milk.
6. Top with whipped cream, a drizzle of caramel sauce and a pinch of sea salt.

The caramel syrup and the brown sugar both sweeten it. For a less sweet latte, halve the brown sugar first, and taste before adding more.

## 2. Gingerbread coffee

The second drink is Allrecipes' Gingerbread Coffee. Stir ½ cup of molasses, ¼ cup of brown sugar, ½ teaspoon of baking soda, 1 teaspoon of ground ginger and ¾ teaspoon of ground cinnamon into a spice mix and chill it for 10 minutes. For each mug, stir 1 tablespoon of the mix into ¼ cup of hot coffee, add ¾ cup more coffee and ¼ cup of half-and-half, and top with whipped cream and a dusting of ground cloves.

Our [gingerbread coffee page](/blog/gingerbread-coffee-a-christmas-miracle/) has the full recipe with weights, how sweet each tablespoon is, how to keep the mix, and our own gingerbread syrup for lattes.

## 3. Toffee coffee

This is Taste of Home's Toffee-Flavored Coffee, which our video makes. Taste of Home writes it for five mugs:

- **Heavy whipping cream:** ½ cup (about 120 g).
- **Confectioners' sugar:** 1 tablespoon (about 8 g).
- **Milk chocolate toffee bits:** ½ cup.
- **Hot brewed coffee:** 5 cups (40 US fl oz, about 1.2 liters).
- **Butterscotch ice cream topping:** 2 tablespoons (about 41 g).
- **Makes:** 5 mugs of about 1 cup each.
- **Time:** about 15 minutes, by Taste of Home.

1. In a small bowl, beat the cream until it begins to thicken.
2. Add the confectioners' sugar and beat until stiff peaks form.
3. Stir the toffee bits into the hot coffee and let it stand for 30 seconds.
4. Strain the coffee and throw away any toffee bits that haven't dissolved.
5. Pour the coffee into mugs, top with the whipped cream and drizzle with the butterscotch topping.

**Two differences in our video.** It makes one mug from 1 cup of coffee but stirs in the whole ½ cup of toffee bits, five times Taste of Home's proportion, so more is left to strain out. And its card for the sugar reads ½ cup, where Taste of Home's recipe and the notes both say 1 tablespoon; use 1 tablespoon. For one mug in Taste of Home's proportions, use 1 cup of coffee and about 1½ tablespoons of toffee bits (calculated).

½ cup of cream whips to about 1 cup (USDA's figures give 2 cups whipped from each cup of heavy cream), enough to top all five mugs. Whipped cream you've sweetened keeps 1 day in the refrigerator, by USDA's FoodKeeper.

## 4. Coffee eggnog punch

Our own recipe, made in a saucepan for four.

- **Coffee ice cream:** 1½ cups.
- **Hot brewed coffee:** 1 cup (8 US fl oz, about 240 ml), brewed strong.
- **Eggnog:** 1½ cups (about 380 g).
- **To top:** 4 tablespoons of whipped topping or whipped cream, and 4 pinches of ground nutmeg.
- **Makes:** 4 small mugs, about 6 to 7 fl oz each (our estimate, since scooped ice cream melts down to less than its volume).
- **Time:** about 5 minutes.

1. Scoop the ice cream into a saucepan over low heat.
2. Stir in the coffee and the eggnog.
3. Heat it for about 3 minutes, stirring, until the ice cream has melted and the punch is hot, without letting it boil.
4. Pour it into 4 mugs or heatproof glasses.
5. Top each with 1 tablespoon of whipped topping and a pinch of nutmeg, and serve straight away.

**Keeping the eggnog.** USDA's FoodKeeper gives store-bought eggnog 3 to 5 days in the refrigerator from the day you buy it. Make the punch to serve, as the notes say, rather than to keep.

## 5. Caramel brûlée latte

The fifth drink is the same recipe as our caramel brûlée latte video: 2 shots of espresso, 1 tablespoon each of caramel syrup and vanilla syrup, 1 cup of milk heated and frothed, and a caramel sauce drizzle on top.

Our [caramel brûlée latte page](/blog/delicious-and-easy-caramel-brulee-latte/) has the full method, a brewed-coffee option for no espresso machine, a darker caramel sauce to make yourself, and what Starbucks says goes into its own Caramel Brulée Latte.

## 6. Spicy coffee and cream

The last drink tops hot coffee with evaporated milk whipped with cinnamon and vanilla. It follows a recipe published on Food.com as "Cinnamon Spiced Coffee," which credits Allrecipes, with half the vanilla.

**For 2 mugs:**

- **Evaporated milk:** ¼ cup (2 fl oz, about 63 g).
- **Confectioners' sugar:** 2¼ teaspoons (about 6 g).
- **Ground cinnamon:** ¼ teaspoon.
- **Vanilla extract:** ⅛ teaspoon. The Food.com recipe uses ¼ teaspoon.
- **Hot brewed coffee:** 1 cup a mug, brewed strong, as on our video's card. The Food.com recipe divides 1 cup between two large mugs, for a creamier, smaller drink.
- **To finish:** ground nutmeg and 2 cinnamon sticks.
- **Time:** about 35 minutes, 30 of them in the freezer.

1. Pour the evaporated milk into a small mixing bowl and put the mixer's beaters in it. Cover the bowl and freeze it for about 30 minutes, until ice crystals start to form.
2. Add the confectioners' sugar, cinnamon and vanilla, and beat until the milk is thick and fluffy.
3. Spoon about ½ cup of the whipped milk into each mug.
4. Pour in the hot coffee and sprinkle with nutmeg.
5. Add a cinnamon stick to each mug and serve straight away.

Use the rest of the can soon: FoodKeeper gives opened evaporated milk 4 to 5 days in the refrigerator.

## Swaps

- **Less sweet:** cut the brown sugar in the latte, or the butterscotch on the toffee coffee, before cutting anything else.
- **Dairy-free:** in the lattes, use a plant milk made for steaming; our [plant milk guide](/blog/best-plant-milks-for-coffee/) compares them. Breville's mocha recipe advises not heating non-dairy milk past 130°F&nbsp;(55°C), as it can separate or curdle.
- **Decaf:** any of these works with decaf coffee or espresso. Decaf still has a little caffeine; our [decaf guide](/blog/how-is-coffee-decaffeinated/) explains how much.

## Caffeine

Our Caffeine Calculator counts the coffee, not the chocolate, the toffee or the coffee ice cream: two shots of espresso, as in the salted mocha caramel latte, come to 120–150&nbsp;mg, and an 8 oz cup of drip coffee to 107–195&nbsp;mg.
