Weighing your coffee and water makes a good cup repeatable, and as James Hoffmann notes, simple digital scales aren't expensive. In October 2020 we evaluated five coffee scales for a video, from a ten-dollar kitchen scale to Hario's pour-over scale, and named a best overall, a best value and a best performer. This page keeps those verdicts, adds what each maker publishes about its scale today, and explains what to look for.

## Why weigh instead of scooping

A scoop measures volume, and coffee doesn't pack the same way twice. In the video we pointed out that density usually falls as roasting goes on, so darker roasts tend to be less dense than lighter ones, and a scoop of a darker roast holds less coffee by weight. Jessica Easto adds in *Craft Coffee* that beans vary a lot in size, so a tablespoon of one coffee can weigh a gram or more different from a tablespoon of another, and that the difference between a perfect cup and a not-as-good one can be half a gram or less.

Weighing also makes troubleshooting easier. Easto's point is that once the ratio is a constant, you can change one other thing, such as the grind, and see what it does. Most recipes on this site give the coffee and the water in grams; our [coffee-to-water ratio guide](/blog/coffee-to-water-ratio-guide/) explains the starting ratios.

## What to look for

- **Resolution.** A 1 g display gives coarser dose control than a 0.1 g display, and resolution alone doesn't establish accuracy. Even on an ideally calibrated scale rounding to the nearest gram, a displayed 25 g could be about 24.5–25.5 g, a ratio of about 1:15.9 to 1:16.6 with 406 g of water (calculated). Espresso doses are smaller and less forgiving: Hoffmann weighs an espresso dose to within 0.1 g of the recipe, Scott Rao's pump-pressure test measures each dose to 0.1 g, and on a machine that stops the shot by volume, he writes, going from 18.0 to 18.5 g of coffee can cut the drink by perhaps 3 to 4 g.
- **How far the fine steps go.** Some coffee scales read 0.1 g only at small weights. Hario's V60 Drip Scale reads 0.1 g up to 200 g, then 0.5 g, then 1 g above 500 g, which is plenty for weighing the water as you pour.
- **A timer.** Useful for pour-over and espresso, where time is part of the recipe. Neither kitchen scale here has one.
- **Auto-off.** A scale that switches itself off after a minute or two can go dark during a slow pour-over. The coffee scales here wait longer: five minutes for the Eravsow and the Hario, which also stays on while its timer runs.
- **Power.** AAA batteries are easy to find; some scales use 3-volt lithium cells, as the Ozeri does, and some coffee scales, such as Acaia's Pearl, charge over USB-C.
- **Water resistance.** Of the makers here, only Hario addresses it: it says its Drip Scale isn't water resistant, so keep spills off it.

## The five scales, our 2020 verdicts and now

The verdicts and observations below are from our 2020 video. The "Now" notes give what each maker publishes about the scale, checked October 18, 2026.

### Eravsow Coffee Scale: our best overall in 2020

Everything we needed in a coffee scale: a large, legible backlit display showing the weight and the brewing time together, readings to 0.1 g, low-battery and overload warnings, two AA batteries included, and a stainless-steel platform that was easy to clean. It switched itself off after five minutes without use. The one downside was that it didn't look or feel premium. Overall, we said, it gave the features that matter at a very reasonable price.

**Now:** Eravsow doesn't publish specs on a website of its own; it sells through retailer listings. The specs here are the ones our video gave in 2020.

### Etekcity Digital Scale: our best value

Stylish and very portable: a stainless-steel platform with a dark, backlit display, about the size of an iPad mini, at around ten dollars in 2020, and it ran on easy-to-find AAA batteries. Two drawbacks: its auto-off made long weighing awkward, and it wasn't as accurate as we'd have liked for very small amounts, around 2 g, though it did better when we weighed them in a container. It has no timer. You won't usually weigh that little coffee, but this is a general kitchen scale that happens to work for coffee.

**Now:** for a current comparison, Etekcity's stainless EK6015 Digital Kitchen Scale was listed at $13.99 on its store when checked October 18, 2026; the exact model of the Etekcity in our 2020 video isn't confirmed. Etekcity lists a 5,000 g (11 lb) capacity, 1 g steps (0.05 oz), two AAA batteries, a backlight that switches off after 30 seconds and power-off after 2 minutes without use, a size of 186 × 145 × 15.5 mm, and a 2-year limited warranty. To buy: [Etekcity's store](https://etekcity.com/products/digital-kitchen-scale-ek6015), or the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Etekcity+EK6015+Digital+Kitchen+Scale&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Etekcity EK6015</a> on Amazon.

### Hario Drip Scale: our best for performance

Made for pour-over, sleek and quite accurate, and we weren't surprised by its performance given Hario's place in specialty coffee. It combines a brewing timer with the weight, has touch-sensitive controls, reads to 0.1 g, and stays on for five minutes, with the auto-off held off while the timer runs. It weighs in grams only. At forty to fifty dollars in 2020 it was hard to recommend to most people despite its performance.

**Now:** our video shows a Hario Drip Scale whose exact model isn't confirmed. Hario USA sells the V60 Drip Scale (product number VSTN-INT-2000B) in black or white, listed at $60 when checked October 18, 2026; Amazon's listing for the earlier Hario Drip Scale Timer points to the VSTN as its newer version. Hario lists a 2,000 g capacity read in 0.1 g steps from 2 to 200 g, 0.5 g to 500 g and 1 g above that, with a stated accuracy of ±0.2 g up to 200 g, ±1 g to 500 g and ±3 g to 2,000 g. Its manual gives two AAA batteries, about a year of use from a set at three two-minute uses a day, and automatic power-off after about 5 minutes, or 99 minutes 59 seconds while the timer runs. Hario says it isn't water resistant. To buy: [Hario USA](https://www.hario-usa.com/products/v60-drip-scale), or the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Hario+V60+Drip+Scale+VSTN-2000B&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Hario V60 Drip Scale</a> on Amazon.

### Ozeri Touch Professional Digital Kitchen Scale

The most stylish of the five: tempered glass in shiny black, touch-sensitive buttons, easy to clean, and one of the most durable-looking scales on the list. It runs on 3-volt lithium batteries, which cost more than AAAs, and it has no timer. When we made the video it cost more than the Etekcity, so we found it hard to recommend as a value.

**Now:** Ozeri lists the Touch Professional Digital Kitchen Scale (12 lbs Edition), model ZK13: 1 g to 5,750 g in 1 g (0.05 oz) steps, tempered glass it calls four times stronger than normal glass, touch buttons, two lithium batteries included, and a size of 9.1 × 5.9 × 0.6 in. Ozeri sells it through retailers and gives no price on its own site. To buy: the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Ozeri+Touch+Professional+Digital+Kitchen+Scale&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Ozeri Touch Professional</a> on Amazon.

### Apexstone Coffee Scale

It had a timer, a backlit display, several units of measure and two AAA batteries included, and it measured reasonably well. But it felt substantially cheaper and flimsier than the others, and it lacked any real sense of style, so with better choices at about the same price, we found it hard to recommend.

**Now:** Apexstone doesn't publish specs on a website of its own; the specs here are from our 2020 video.

## A premium option: Acaia Pearl

The Acaia Pearl wasn't in our 2020 video. Acaia's current Pearl, listed at $150 on its store when checked October 18, 2026, reads 0.1 g across its whole 2,000 g range, connects over Bluetooth 5.0, shows a real-time flow-rate indicator for pour-over, and charges over USB-C, with 30 to 40 hours of continuous use per charge by Acaia's figure. Acaia gives a 2-year warranty in the US and EU. It suits you if you want Bluetooth or a flow-rate guide; the scale's basic job, weight and time, is the same as Hario's.

## Our 2020 verdict

For most people we recommended the Eravsow, which covered the essentials, timer included, at a low price. The Etekcity was our pick for the money, as long as you don't need a timer, and the Hario performed best but cost enough to put most people off. Today, Etekcity's EK6015 and Hario's V60 Drip Scale are still sold with the specs above, which makes the choice simpler: a 1 g kitchen scale you already own is a useful step up from scoops for filter coffee, if you're happy to time it on your phone; for a new purchase, Easto recommends 0.1 g readability, and a 0.1 g scale with a timer suits daily pour-over and espresso.
