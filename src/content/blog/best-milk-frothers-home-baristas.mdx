A milk frother has up to three jobs: adding air, making the bubbles fine, and heating the milk. Frothers split them differently. A handheld adds air but leaves the heating to you; an electric jug does everything in one cycle; a steam wand, on a machine or a stovetop steamer, heats and aerates with steam. This page compares seven frothers by what each is built to do, from their makers' own specifications, and doesn't rank them.

## Foam or microfoam: what do you need?

James Hoffmann describes great milk foam as like liquid marshmallow: bubbles so small they're almost invisible, which he and others call microfoam. It's elastic and pourable. Scott Rao's goal for steamed milk is the same: a tight micro-bubble structure, with a glassy surface and no visible bubbles. That's the milk latte art needs. For pouring latte art, Rao wants milk that looks "creamy and glassy, with no visible bubbles."

How much foam a frother makes is a different measure from how fine it is. Lab studies of milk foam often report foam volume and how long the foam lasts, and a tall head of foam can still be made of big bubbles. For a cappuccino, Rao wants very aerated milk, with about ½&nbsp;inch (about 13&nbsp;mm) of foam once the drink settles; for a flat white, only a skin of foam. So decide first:

- **Latte art, or a flat white:** you need fine, glossy, pourable foam. Judge any frother by Rao's test: a glassy surface with no visible bubbles.
- **A foamy cappuccino or a quick hot milk drink:** plenty of warm foam may be all you want, and convenience may matter more than texture.

## How hot should the milk get?

Hoffmann says milk begins to degrade irreversibly in flavor and texture above 154°F&nbsp;(68°C), and finds a very hot cappuccino lacks the texture, flavor and sweetness of one made with milk heated only to 140°F&nbsp;(60°C). Breville's Bambino espresso machine manual puts steamed milk at 140–149°F&nbsp;(60–65°C); Rao's café target is 150–160°F&nbsp;(66–71°C), and the top of that is past Hoffmann's limit. Our [milk steaming guide](/blog/how-to-steam-milk-for-lattes/) explains the differences.

The frothers' makers publish very different amounts of detail on this:

- **Subminimal's NanoFoamer** doesn't heat milk. Subminimal says to warm it to 131–149°F&nbsp;(55–65°C) first, on the stove or in the microwave; it gives the Celsius figures, and the Fahrenheit is calculated.
- **Breville's Milk Café** has a dial from Cold Stir through Warm, drawn in its US manual with marks at 120°, 140° and 160° and a band labeled "Optimum Milk Temp" around 140°, which Breville recommends for cappuccinos and lattes.
- **Nespresso's Aeroccino3** heats milk to between 160 and 170°F&nbsp;(71–77°C, calculated), Nespresso says, which is above Hoffmann's 154°F limit.
- **Nespresso's Aeroccino4** and **Instant Pot's Milk Frother** don't give a temperature on their product pages.
- **Bellman's CX25P** is a steam wand, so you decide when to stop.

## For latte art without an espresso machine

### Subminimal NanoFoamer V2 and NanoFoamer Lithium

The NanoFoamer is a handheld foamer with an impeller: instead of a wire whisk, it spins the milk through a clip-on screen Subminimal calls a NanoScreen, and it comes with a Fine and a Superfine screen. Subminimal calls it "the best microfoamed milk you can produce without a steam wand" and says the milk has "the texture and consistency needed for professional latte art." Those are the maker's claims; Rao's glassy-surface test is the way to check them in your own kitchen.

It doesn't heat the milk. Subminimal's instructions are to warm the milk to 131–149°F&nbsp;(55–65°C) first, then put at least 1.5&nbsp;in (3.8&nbsp;cm) in a narrow jug, aerate near the surface for 3 to 20 seconds, then move the head deeper and slightly off-center to churn the foam smooth. For latte art it says to use warm milk, since cold milk foams too thick to pour designs. Cold milk works for cold foam on iced drinks.

The V2 runs on two AA batteries and has a single-speed motor, whatever some retailer listings say; the Lithium is rechargeable over USB-C and has two speeds. Both are IPX-4 splash resistant: rinse the shaft and screens under the tap, don't submerge the handle, and keep it out of the dishwasher. Subminimal's own store listed the V2 at $37 and the Lithium at $49.95 when checked October 18, 2026. To buy: [Subminimal](https://subminimal.com/products/nanofoamer), or the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Subminimal+NanoFoamer+V2&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">NanoFoamer V2</a> or <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Subminimal+NanoFoamer+Lithium&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">NanoFoamer Lithium</a> on Amazon.

### Bellman CX25P

The Bellman CX25P is a stovetop pressure brewer with a steam wand and a pressure gauge: it brews 3 to 9 cups of what Bellman calls espresso, and steams milk through the wand. Bellman says the wand makes "cafe-quality microfoam." Because it's a steam wand, the technique is the same as on an espresso machine, so our [steaming guide](/blog/how-to-steam-milk-for-lattes/) applies.

Bellman lists 18/8 stainless steel, use on gas, electric and ceramic stoves and "some" induction cooktops, hand cleaning only, and a one-year warranty. Bellman's US store listed it at $199.95, against a regular price of $289.00, when checked October 18, 2026. To buy: [Bellman](https://bellmanespresso.com/products/bellman-cx25p-espresso-and-milk-steamer), or the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Bellman+CX-25P+Espresso+Steamer&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Bellman CX25P</a> on Amazon.

## For hot foam at the press of a button

Electric jugs whisk and heat the milk in one cycle. None of the three makers below claims latte art; they sell their jugs for drinks like cappuccinos and lattes, and for cold foam.

### Nespresso Aeroccino4

The Aeroccino4 has four settings: cold milk foam, airy hot milk foam, dense hot milk foam and hot milk with no foam. It froths up to 4.06&nbsp;oz (120&nbsp;ml) and heats up to 8.11&nbsp;oz (240&nbsp;ml) of milk, and Nespresso lists it as dishwasher safe. Nespresso doesn't give its temperature on the product page. Nespresso's US store listed it at $129.00 when checked October 18, 2026. Amazon's search for it returns the Aeroccino3 instead, so buy it from [Nespresso](https://www.nespresso.com/us/en/order/accessories/vertuo/aeroccino-4-milk-frother-new).

### Nespresso Aeroccino3

The Aeroccino3 has one button and three settings: a one-second press for hot milk or hot milk foam, and a two-second press for cold milk froth. It holds 4.1&nbsp;oz for foam and 8.1&nbsp;oz for hot milk. Nespresso says it heats milk to between 160 and 170°F, hotter than the 140°F that Hoffmann prefers and past his 154°F limit; by his account, very hot milk gives up some texture, flavor and sweetness. Its non-stick inside is not dishwasher safe. Nespresso's US store listed it at $99.00 when checked October 18, 2026. To buy: [Nespresso](https://www.nespresso.com/us/en/order/accessories/vertuo/aeroccino3-black), or the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Nespresso+Aeroccino+3&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Nespresso Aeroccino 3</a> on Amazon.

### Breville Milk Café

The Milk Café (BMF600XL) is the largest jug here. It heats by induction and spins a frothing disc at the bottom of the jug: Breville's "Capp" disc is "for thick and creamy froth" and its "Latté" disc "for smooth and silky milk." Fill it with cold milk between the MIN and MAX marks; for froth, Breville says not to go past the mark it labels "16 fl oz / 500 ml", which it says makes about two to three cappuccinos, while the 8&nbsp;fl&nbsp;oz (250&nbsp;ml) mark makes one or two. A Cold Stir setting stirs without heating. A cycle takes 2 to 7 minutes, depending on how much milk you use.

The jug, lid, measuring cap and discs can go on the dishwasher's top shelf, though Breville recommends hand-washing the discs to make them last; the base is wiped clean. It's 500 W, with a one-year limited warranty. Breville's US store listed it at $199.95 when checked October 18, 2026. To buy: [Breville](https://www.breville.com/us/en/products/milk-frothers/bmf600.html), or the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Breville+Milk+Cafe+BMF600XL&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Breville Milk Café BMF600XL</a> on Amazon.

### Instant Pot Milk Frother

Instant Pot's 4-in-1 Milk Frother makes cold foam, light warm foam and dense warm foam, or simply warms milk. Instant Pot says it froths up to 8.5&nbsp;oz and heats up to 17&nbsp;oz of milk, though its spec list also gives a 10&nbsp;oz volume, and Amazon's listing says 10&nbsp;oz (295&nbsp;ml). Instant Pot says it "creates rich microfoam from dairy and non-dairy beverages." The inside is non-stick, the lid and whisk are dishwasher safe, and the base, which plugs in, is not; it has a one-year limited warranty. Instant Pot's US store listed it at $49.99, in black or white, when checked October 18, 2026. To buy: [Instant Pot](https://instantpot.com/products/instant-pot-milk-frother-white), or the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Instant+Pot+Milk+Frother+4-in-1&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Instant Pot 4-in-1 Milk Frother</a> on Amazon.

## How much milk does one drink need?

Check a frother's capacity against the drinks you make. In the published recipes our Coffee Drinks tool draws, La Cimbali's cappuccino uses 125&nbsp;ml of frothed milk and illy's caffè latte 120&nbsp;ml of milk, measured before heating. The Aeroccinos' 120&nbsp;ml foam limit covers about one of those; for two at once, you need a bigger jug or two cycles.

## If you already have a steam wand

A steam wand on an espresso machine heats and aerates with steam, as the Bellman does, so if you have one, learn it before buying a frother. Our [milk steaming guide](/blog/how-to-steam-milk-for-lattes/) has the method, and the [latte art guide](/blog/latte-art-beginners-guide/) takes it from the pitcher to the cup.

## Plant milks and frothers

How a plant milk is foamed can matter as much as which milk it is. In Tarasov and colleagues' 2025 tests, barista plant drinks steamed at 149°F&nbsp;(65°C) were all workable alternatives to cow's milk for a cappuccino, but not when they were foamed by mechanical agitation instead of steam. Whisks and jugs foam mechanically, so if you use a plant milk in one, choose a carton its maker sells for frothing, and check the foam. Our [plant milk guide](/blog/best-plant-milks-for-coffee/) compares the cartons, and the [oat milk vs dairy](/blog/oat-milk-vs-dairy-in-coffee/) comparison covers foam tests with an electric frother.

## Serve it soon

However you froth, foamed milk separates as it sits: the liquid drains down and the foam rises. Rao pours within 30 seconds of steaming, because the texture degrades with time even if you keep the milk moving. Time the milk to finish with the espresso, or just before it.
