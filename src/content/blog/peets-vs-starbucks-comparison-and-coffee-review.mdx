## How Peet's and Starbucks are related

Alfred Peet opened his shop at the corner of Vine and Walnut streets in Berkeley, California, on April 1, 1966. Peet's describes his roasting as "a sensory roasting technique calibrated to achieve a dark, sweet, even roast."

In 1971, three young entrepreneurs came to Peet to learn the trade. Peet's history says he trained them for two years and supplied roasted beans to their new company, Starbucks, until 1973, when Peet's stopped supplying them and urged them to roast their own. HistoryLink, Washington State's online encyclopedia, names the three as Jerry Baldwin, Gordon Bowker and Zev Siegl, and dates their first store, near Seattle's Pike Place Market, to March 30, 1971.

The two companies came together again in 1984, when Starbucks bought Peet's. By 1987 Baldwin had decided that Peet's was where he wanted to be, and Starbucks was sold to a group of investors led by Howard Schultz, according to HistoryLink. One person links the two roasteries directly: Jim Reynolds joined Starbucks as its roaster in July 1973 (HistoryLink) and became Peet's second roastmaster in 1984 (Peet's). His JR Reserve Blend is our top Peet's pick below.

Today Peet's says it has "several hundred coffeebars." Starbucks reported 40,990 stores worldwide at the end of its 2025 fiscal year, on September 28, 2025.

## Roast styles

Both chains are known for dark roasts, and in our 2020 tasting both tasted darker than coffee from most specialty roasters. All five of our Peet's picks are coffees Peet's sells as dark roasts. Starbucks sorts its whole-bean coffees into Blonde, Medium and Dark Roast, and our five Starbucks picks cover all three.

The difference we noticed was within each range. Peet's coffees tasted more distinct from one another, as if each was roasted for that coffee; Starbucks' tasted more alike, shades of one approach.

## Caffeine: what each chain publishes

Starbucks lists a grande (16 fl oz) Pike Place Roast at 315–390 mg of caffeine, and gives the same approximate range for its brewed Blonde Roast (Starbucks Sunsera) and its featured dark roast (Starbucks 1971 Roast). Peet's gives about 100–200 mg for a 6 oz cup of brewed coffee. Scaled to 16 fl oz, that's about 265–535 mg (calculated: multiply by 16 ÷ 6).

The ranges overlap, so neither chain's brewed coffee is reliably stronger. At home, the caffeine in your cup depends mostly on how much coffee you use. Roast matters less than it's often said to: our [caffeine guide](/blog/caffeine-in-coffee-guide/) covers what changes with roast, and the FDA's guidance on daily amounts.

## Our five Peet's picks (2020)

These are the five Peet's coffees from our 2020 video, in the order we ranked them. The tasting notes are ours. The descriptions are Peet's own, as published on its website when checked October 15, 2026. All five were listed there, though the House Blend's pound bag showed as sold out that day.

### 1. <a href="https://www.peets.com/products/half-pound-jr-reserve-blend">Peet's JR Reserve Blend</a>

**As published by Peet's:** a dark-roast signature blend that Roastmaster Emeritus Jim Reynolds put together from "very rare lots known for both their consistency and complexity," with flavor notes of rum, complex spice and cocoa. It comes in half-pound bags: listed at about $35 a half pound, roughly three times Major Dickason's price per pound, when checked October 15, 2026.

**What we found:** full-bodied, with prominent chocolate and a spice we kept trying to name, something peppery and something like cinnamon. Our favorite Peet's coffee, and an occasional splurge at that price.

### 2. <a href="https://www.peets.com/products/guatemala-san-sebastian">Peet's Guatemala San Sebastián</a>

**As published by Peet's:** a washed, dark-roasted single origin from a farm in Guatemala's Antigua valley, "a favorite of our Roastmasters for more than 40 years," with notes of vanilla orchid, cocoa and guayaba pastille.

**What we found:** bittersweet chocolate, clean and bright acidity, and natural sweetness, with the coffee's own character still showing through the dark roast. Try it as a pour-over to taste the chocolate and fruit.

### 3. <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Peets+Major+Dickasons+Blend&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Peet's Major Dickason's Blend</a>

**As published by Peet's:** its "all-time best seller," a dark-roasted blend of Latin American and Indo-Pacific coffees "suggested by loyal customer Key Dickason and perfected by Mr. Peet," with flavor notes of earth, spice and "everything." Peet's history dates Dickason's idea to 1969.

**What we found:** rich, smooth and complex: earthy and nutty, with an underlying sweetness. It works well in drip, a press pot or espresso.

### 4. <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Peets+French+Roast+Whole+Bean+Coffee&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Peet's French Roast</a>

**As published by Peet's:** "our darkest roast," with "smoky overtones, a pleasant bite, and surprising sweetness," and flavor notes of dark chocolate, smoke and caramel brûlée.

**What we found:** bold and smoky, with a heavy body. Excellent with milk and sugar, but worth trying black for the complexity underneath. One of the better French roasts from any big brand.

### 5. <a href="https://www.peets.com/products/house-blend">Peet's House Blend</a>

**As published by Peet's:** a dark-roasted Latin American blend with flavor notes of spice, citrus and toast, which Peet's calls "your perfect introduction to Peet's coffees."

**What we found:** bright, with a hint of spice and a crisp finish. Less full-bodied than the others, and a good first Peet's coffee.

## Our five Starbucks picks (2020)

The same approach: our 2020 tasting notes, with Starbucks' own descriptions from its website when checked October 15, 2026. Four of the five are in Starbucks' current whole-bean range.

### 1. <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Starbucks+Pike+Place+Roast+Coffee&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Starbucks Pike Place Roast</a>

**As published by Starbucks:** a medium roast created in 2008, "a smooth, well-rounded blend of Latin American coffees with subtly rich flavors of chocolate and toasted nuts."

**What we found:** smooth and balanced, with nuts and chocolate. It's hard to dislike, which is the point, and it was the most consistent, best-made coffee in Starbucks' lineup.

### 2. <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Starbucks+Sumatra+Dark+Roast+Coffee&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Starbucks Sumatra</a>

**As published by Starbucks:** a dark-roast single origin from Indonesia, "strikingly bold and full-bodied with rich herbals, rustic spice notes and a muted acidity." Starbucks says it has sold Sumatran coffee since 1971.

**What we found:** earthy, with less smokiness than most Sumatras, minimal acidity and a full body. A toned-down Sumatra that's approachable if you're new to it.

### 3. Starbucks Brazil blend

**What we found:** maple, vanilla, bright and fruity. It was harder to find even in 2020, and it defied the "burnt Starbucks" stereotype.

We couldn't find this coffee in Starbucks' whole-bean range on its website on October 15, 2026, or confirm its exact name, so there's no description or link.

### 4. <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Starbucks+Veranda+Blend+Coffee&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Starbucks Veranda Blend</a>

**As published by Starbucks:** a Blonde Roast of Latin American coffees, "mellow and flavorful with a nice softness." Starbucks says it took "more than 80 tries to get it right."

**What we found:** buttery, sweet and mild. A good way in if you prefer lighter coffee.

### 5. <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Starbucks+Cafe+Verona+Coffee&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Starbucks Caffè Verona</a>

**As published by Starbucks:** a dark roast first blended for a Seattle restaurant in 1975 as Jake's Blend, then sold in Starbucks stores as the 80/20 Blend before it became Verona.

**What we found:** chocolate, nuts, caramel and low acidity. Not adventurous, but reliable and pleasant.

## Our verdict (2020)

**Peet's had the edge for home brewing.** Its coffees showed more variety and more complexity, and its roasting seemed more deliberate, coffee by coffee. The JR Reserve and the Guatemala San Sebastián were excellent, and would hold up well against mid-tier specialty roasters.

**Starbucks wins on accessibility and consistency.** Pike Place is a well-made crowd-pleaser you can buy almost anywhere, and the Blonde Roasts show the range has broadened. But the core lineup felt more uniform: you're choosing between shades of the same approach rather than different experiences.

If you're used to a specialty roaster, both chains will taste dark. If you're coming from supermarket coffee, both are a step up. For a lighter, origin-forward cup, the real comparison is with a local specialty roaster's fresh single origin, not between these two. Buying from a grocery shelf, we'd pick Peet's.
