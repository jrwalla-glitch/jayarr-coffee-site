Slow-drip cold brew, sometimes called Kyoto-style, usually takes a tower: a bowl of ice water on top, a tap that lets it drip through a bed of coffee, and a pot below. The PuckPuck shrinks that to a valve that sits on an AeroPress. It's made by PuckPuck Ltd., which says it isn't affiliated with or endorsed by AeroPress. The product details and brewing method below are PuckPuck's own, from its website when checked October 18, 2026; the verdict is from our 2020 video.

## What it is

PuckPuck's product page describes:

- **The PuckPuck valve,** a two-part plastic screw valve that PuckPuck calls patent pending. It's 2.4 cm tall and 6.8 cm wide, and twisting it sets the drip rate.
- **A splash filter,** which sits on top of the grounds inside the AeroPress so the drips spread across the coffee instead of landing in one spot, and clips onto the valve for storage.
- **A 500 ml water vessel** with a lid, which screws into the valve. The valve's thread also fits some mineral water bottles, such as Evian or Volvic with the base cut off, which is how the cheaper PuckPuck Solo is meant to be used.
- **A free drip-counter app** for Apple and Android: tap along with the drips and it tells you whether to speed up or slow down.

The parts are food-grade, BPA-free plastic. PuckPuck says it fits "any AeroPress Coffee Maker" and doesn't list models, so it doesn't say how it fits AeroPress's newer sizes.

**Availability.** In 2020 our video paid about £30 ($35) for the PuckPuck with its vessel. When checked October 18, 2026, PuckPuck's own store listed it at £29.95 but sold out, along with the Solo, the spare parts and everything else in the store. PuckPuck's stockists page still names shops around the world, including Espresso Parts in the US; we didn't find the PuckPuck in a search on Amazon. Check with a stockist before you plan on one.

## PuckPuck's method

- **Coffee:** 38 g, ground medium
- **Water:** 400 ml of water and 100 g of ice, 500 g in all. That's about 1:13 (calculated: 500 ÷ 38).
- **Time:** about 10 minutes to set up, then 2½ to 3 hours of dripping
- **Makes:** about 424 g, or about 14½ US fl oz (calculated: 500 g of water, less about 76 g held back by the grounds at roughly 2 g per gram of coffee, our Brew Guide's working figure for immersion brews; a drip bed may hold back a little less or more)

1. Unclip the splash filter and set it aside. Unscrew the two halves of the PuckPuck, rinse them with hot water, shake them dry and check that all 4 vent holes are clear, then screw it back together, clockwise, until closed.
2. Put a paper filter in the AeroPress cap, screw it on and stand the AeroPress on a jug that holds at least 500 ml.
3. Add 38 g of medium-ground coffee, shake it level and drop the splash filter on top of the grounds.
4. Screw the water vessel into the PuckPuck and set them on top of the AeroPress. Add 100 g of ice to the vessel, then 400 ml of water.
5. Turn the PuckPuck slowly counterclockwise until it starts to drip, then set the rate to about 50 drops a minute, using the drip-counter app if you like.
6. Leave it for 2½ to 3 hours, until the vessel is empty, then lift off the PuckPuck and vessel.

**Use a big enough jug.** The jug has to hold all 400 ml of water plus the melted ice. We overflowed on our first attempt in the video by brewing straight into a small glass.

**The drip rate drifts.** PuckPuck says the drip method is sensitive to room temperature and humidity, so adjust the rate to finish in 2½ to 3 hours rather than holding exactly 50. In our video the first setting ran at 94 drops a minute; after a few turns it settled at 61, which we treated as on target for the 50 to 60 we were aiming at. PuckPuck's other fixes:

- **The drips stop:** use ice water. PuckPuck says cold water keeps the air bubbles passing through the filter small, so they don't interrupt the flow.
- **Erratic drips or flooding:** water is blocking the side vent holes; a quick blow into them should clear it.
- **Grounds still dry at the end:** coffee more than about 30 days past its roast date may need pre-wetting. Pour 30 to 40 ml of water over the grounds first, and put that much less in the vessel.

PuckPuck adds that an AeroPress paper filter, trimmed by about 3 mm, can stand in for the splash filter, and that a metal AeroPress filter works in the cap instead of paper.

## How strong it is, and how long it keeps

At about 1:13, this is stronger than our Brew Guide's hot V60 at 1:16 and close to Hario's slow-drip tower recipe, about 12 g of coffee per 140 ml of water, or 1:11.7 (calculated). It's much lighter than a cold brew concentrate, which the Brew Guide makes at 1:5.5 to 1:7. PuckPuck doesn't give a dilution: it suggests the coffee over ice, with milk or as a cocktail mixer. Our video found it "beautifully strong," with no sign of being watered down, so drink it as it is and add milk or ice to taste.

PuckPuck's FAQ says slow-drip coffee "tastes better fresh but can be stored for up to two weeks" in an airtight container in the fridge, and our video gave about two weeks too. Hario is more cautious about the coffee from its Water Dripper tower: about 3 days in its lidded pot in the fridge. Neither figure comes from a published study. Our [Japanese iced coffee vs cold brew](/blog/japanese-iced-coffee-vs-cold-brew/) comparison has Hario's full method.

## The results, in 2020

Three hours later we had a dark cold brew from Taylors of Harrogate's Lazy Sunday, which the video calls light, gentle and easygoing. It tasted of dark chocolate with beautiful roast notes, a little sweeter than the same beans brewed hot. The mouthfeel was smooth and creamy, crisp and clean but full of flavor: full-bodied, strong and refreshing, with no wateriness or thinness. The balance was what impressed us most.

Cleanup was the one fiddly part. PuckPuck's method is to lift off the valve and vessel, tap the AeroPress and unscrew the cap so the grounds come out with the splash filter on top, take the splash filter off before you throw the grounds away, and rinse everything under warm water. That's more to deal with than an ordinary AeroPress brew.

## Our verdict (2020)

At about £30 ($35), the price listed when our 2020 video bought it, the PuckPuck was one of the best coffee accessories we'd tested. It was easy to use, it worked with an AeroPress we already owned, and it made excellent slow-drip cold brew in about three hours, with no tower and no overnight wait. If you can find one in stock, it's still an easy way into slow drip at home. If you can't, a jar of [cold brew concentrate](/blog/cold-brew-concentrate-guide/) makes a different, stronger drink, and our [AeroPress guide](/blog/why-and-how-you-should-use-an-aeropress/) and the [Instant Pot method](/blog/cold-brew-coffeewith-an-instant-pot/) are other ways to get coffee for iced drinks.
