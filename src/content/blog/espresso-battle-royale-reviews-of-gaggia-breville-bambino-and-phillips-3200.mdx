These three machines represent three fundamentally different philosophies about home espresso. The Gaggia Classic Pro says: learn the craft, tinker, grow. The Breville Bambino Plus says: get good results now, with the machine doing the hardest part. The Philips 3200 says: press a button, drink coffee, don't think about it. All three philosophies are valid. The question is which one matches how you actually want to spend your mornings.

## What has changed since the video

- **Gaggia Classic Pro.** Gaggia North America now sells the Classic Pro E24 as "the newest version" of the machine; Gaggia's own site, and our chooser, call it the Classic E24. The data behind our Espresso Machine Chooser records the 2019 Classic Pro and the Classic Evo Pro as replaced generations. One difference you can't see: Gaggia's service manual for the 2019 Classic and the Evo Pro lists an aluminum boiler, while the E24 has a lead-free brass one.
- **Breville Bambino Plus.** The same model, BES500, is still on Breville's US site.
- **Philips 3200.** Philips's US page for the 3200 LatteGo, EP3241/54, says "this product is no longer available," and the chooser's data doesn't name a successor. The specs below are for that model.

Our video compares the machines as they were sold in early 2021. The specs and prices below are the makers' current ones, checked on October 15, 2026, so where the video and the text differ, follow the text.

## Semi-automatic or super-automatic

The Gaggia and the Breville are **semi-automatic** machines. You grind your own beans, dose them into a portafilter, tamp, and start and stop the shot. The machine supplies the water's pressure and temperature, but you do the rest, so your technique shows in the cup: there's a learning curve, and room to keep improving.

The Philips 3200 is a **super-automatic.** It grinds, doses and brews at the press of a button, and with its LatteGo carafe it froths the milk too. You give up control of the dose, the shot and most of the grind, and in return you skip the learning curve.

That split matters more than any single spec. Choose your routine first, then the machine.

## Gaggia Classic Pro, now the E24

**As published by Gaggia and its US importer:** a 58 mm stainless steel filter holder; a three-way solenoid valve that releases the pressure after the shot and leaves drier pucks; a single lead-free brass boiler; a 15-bar pump with the over-pressure valve set to 9 bar; and a 2.1 L tank. It comes with one pressurized filter for one or two cups and traditional filters for one and two cups, and Gaggia North America gives filter capacities of 7 to 18 g. Gaggia North America listed it at about $550 in stainless steel and $600 in colors when checked October 15, 2026.

**Temperature.** Neither Gaggia page we checked lists PID temperature control. Instead, the manual sets a routine. Before the first coffee, run a cup of water through the filter holder and wait a few minutes for the machine to reach temperature. After steaming milk, run a cup of water through the brew group to bring the boiler back down before the next coffee; otherwise, Gaggia warns, it may taste burnt.

**Milk.** Gaggia's product page lists a professional stainless steel steam wand, and Gaggia North America a commercial-style wand with a two-hole tip. Gaggia's manual describes "a steam wand just like professional machines," and the chooser records it as a manual steam wand. With one boiler, you brew, then steam, one after the other.

**Care.** Gaggia recommends cleaning the brew group monthly with its Coffee Oil Remover tablets and a blind filter, both sold separately, and descaling every 2 months with Gaggia's own descaler only: "Never use vinegar or other descaling agents."

**Who it suits:** someone who wants to do every step, likes the idea of a 58 mm portafilter and a three-way valve, and will buy an espresso grinder too.

## Breville Bambino Plus (BES500)

The Bambino Plus is the machine we find ourselves recommending more than any other to people who ask "what should I buy?" Not because it's the best machine — the Gaggia arguably pulls better shots in skilled hands — but because it removes nearly every barrier between you and good espresso.

**As published by Breville:** a ThermoJet heating system that reaches extraction temperature in 3 seconds; low-pressure pre-infusion followed by 9-bar extraction; a 54 mm portafilter with single- and dual-wall baskets for one and two cups; an 18 g dose; PID temperature control; and automatic milk texturing with "3 adjustable milk temperatures and 3 levels of texture," or "hands on manual texturing like a barista." Breville's manual gives a 1.9 L tank. Breville listed it at about $500 when checked October 15, 2026.

**Milk.** Here's why the automatic wand matters: steaming milk well takes practice, and on this machine you can set the jug under the wand, press a button and let the machine texture it. The three temperature settings are for the milk, not the brew water.

**Size.** Breville suggests 12 in of countertop clearance. We don't quote its printed width and depth, because Breville's listed sizes conflict with its own clearance guidance.

**Care.** The machine tells you when a cleaning cycle is due, after 200 extractions, and you run it with a cleaning disc and one cleaning tablet. Descaling is a separate cycle, signaled when the 1 CUP+STEAM and 2 CUP buttons flash alternately; Breville gives no fixed interval.

**The trade-offs:** a 54 mm portafilter rather than 58 mm, and no grinder, so you'll need an espresso grinder alongside it.

## Philips 3200 LatteGo (EP3241/54)

**As published by Philips:** a ceramic grinder with 12 settings; five drinks (espresso, coffee, cappuccino, latte macchiato and Americano) plus hot water; a LatteGo milk system of "2 parts, no tubes" that Philips says rinses clean in as little as 15 seconds under the tap or goes in the dishwasher; a 15-bar pump; a 1.8 L tank; and a brew group you remove and rinse under the tap. Its size is 246 mm wide, 433 mm deep and 371 mm high, about 9.7 × 17.0 × 14.6 in (calculated). Philips's US page shows no price and says the model is no longer available.

**Care.** Philips lists guided descaling, and says its AquaClean water filter allows up to 5,000 cups without descaling if you change the filter when the machine prompts (its footnote assumes eight filter changes, and says actual counts vary with your drinks, rinsing and cleaning).

**The trade-offs:** you can't choose the dose, time the shot or grind beyond its 12 settings. If you want this routine today, compare current super-automatics; the chooser's "Push a button" setting suggests them.

## The routine each one asks of you

Our Brew Guide starts a double espresso at 18 g of coffee for 36 g in the cup, in about 25 to 30 seconds. The two semi-automatics work from a recipe like that; the Philips sets its own.

**Gaggia Classic Pro E24:**
1. Turn it on, run a cup of water through the filter holder, and wait for the temperature light.
2. Grind and dose, up to the 18 g Gaggia North America gives for its filters, then tamp level.
3. Lock in the portafilter, start the shot and the timer together, and stop at your target weight.
4. To steam milk, switch to steam; before the next coffee, run a cup of water through the group to bring the boiler back down.

**Breville Bambino Plus:**
1. Turn it on; Breville says it's ready in 3 seconds.
2. Grind and dose 18 g into the 54 mm basket, then tamp.
3. Lock in the portafilter and press a cup button; pre-infusion runs first.
4. Set the jug under the wand and press for automatic milk, or steam by hand.

**Philips 3200 LatteGo:**
1. Turn it on and choose a drink on the display.
2. Press the button; it grinds, doses and brews.
3. For milk drinks, clip on the LatteGo carafe.

## Which one to choose

**Choose the Gaggia Classic Pro E24 if** you want to learn espresso as a skill, want a 58 mm portafilter and a three-way valve, and are ready to buy a good grinder separately. It's listed just over $500 (checked October 15, 2026), before the grinder. Our [guide to espresso machines listed under $500](/blog/best-espresso-machines-under-500-2026-7-picks-for-every-budget/) compares it with cheaper machines.

**Choose the Breville Bambino Plus if** most of your drinks have milk and you want the machine to texture it, while you still make the shot yourself. You'll need an espresso grinder too.

**Choose a super-automatic if** you want coffee at a button, with no grinder to buy and nothing to learn. The 3200 LatteGo is no longer sold by Philips in the US, so start from the makers' current models.

**The choice boils down to this:** do you want to learn espresso as a skill (Gaggia), milk drinks without learning to steam (Bambino Plus), or coffee at a button press (a super-automatic)? Each answer is legitimate.
