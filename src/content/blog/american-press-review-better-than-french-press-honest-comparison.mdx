## What the American Press is

The American Press won a Red Dot product design award in 2018, in the coffee and tea maker category. Red Dot lists the maker as American Press LLC of Brooklyn, New York, and the designers as Alexander Albanese and Jeff Bull, and its jury liked the "simple and clever idea" of giving the grounds their own brewing chamber. It's sold under the brand name It's American Press, as the box in our video showed.

It has three parts: a carafe that holds the water, a pod that holds the coffee behind a steel filter, and the plunger that pushes the pod down. You fill the carafe, push the pod in until the water just covers the coffee, let it steep, then press the pod slowly to the bottom. The water passes up through the coffee and the filter, so the brewed coffee ends up above the pod and the grounds stay sealed inside it. In our video you can watch it happen: coffee on top, clear water still underneath.

**As published by the maker** on its Amazon listing (the brand's own store), checked October 18, 2026:

- **Capacity:** 12 fl oz. The box in our 2020 video said up to 14 fl oz; the max fill line we brewed to held about 355 ml, which is 12 fl oz.
- **Filter:** a 100-micron, laser-etched steel filter in a reusable pod, so no paper.
- **Carafe:** double-walled, shatter-resistant Tritan (a plastic), which the maker says keeps drinks hot longer.
- **Method:** about 20 g of medium to medium-fine coffee; press the pod just far enough to wet the coffee, wait 30 to 60 seconds, then press and pour. The listing says it works with loose-leaf tea, too.

## How we brewed it (2020)

We followed the instructions in the box, which differed a little from today's listing:

- **Coffee:** a medium-roast Costa Rican, at a medium grind. The instructions said 20 to 24 g, but we found the pod held only about 15 to 20 g. A grind that's too fine makes the press hard to push.
- **Water:** filled to the max line, about 355 ml. The maker gave no water temperature, so we used 199°F&nbsp;(93°C), a common choice for immersion brewing. At 15 to 20 g of coffee that's about 1:18 to 1:24 (calculated: 355 ÷ 20 and 355 ÷ 15), a lighter ratio than the maker's 20 to 24 g would make.
- **Steep:** we pushed the pod in until the first coffee appeared above it, then waited 30 seconds. The instructions allowed anywhere from 30 seconds to 2 minutes.
- **Press:** about 2 minutes, at the slow end of the instructions' 30 seconds to 2 minutes. If the press resists, lift it a couple of inches and carry on, as with a French press.

The cup looked a little light to us. It tasted pleasant, with no unpleasant woody notes, but we knew from brewing these beans in an AeroPress that there was more flavor in them than this cup showed. It poured without spilling, as the box promised.

## Three brewers, one coffee

Next we made three cups from the same beans, at the same medium grind and the same 199°F&nbsp;(93°C) water: one each in the American Press, an AeroPress and a French press. The cups weren't hidden from the taster, so this was a side-by-side tasting, not a blind one.

- **French press:** a slightly woody note, which we took as a sign it had steeped too long. Not unpleasant, but these beans can taste a lot better.
- **AeroPress:** our everyday brewer at the time, and it tasted like a different coffee, with fruity flavors coming through.
- **American Press:** better than we expected. It still got a lot of the flavor out of the beans.

| Brewer | Flavor (2020) | Cleanup (2020) |
|---|---|---|
| AeroPress | 1st | 2nd: a few parts to clean, and the paper and grounds to throw out |
| American Press | 2nd | 1st: the pod lifts out and empties easily |
| French press | 3rd | 3rd: grounds stick in the bottom and end up all over the sink |

Two things to keep in mind. The grind suited the American Press and wasn't tuned for each brewer: AeroPress recommends medium-fine for its own method, and our Brew Guide grinds a French press extra coarse. And the cleanup ranking is the American Press's main argument. A French press is easy to leave dirty for days; the American Press's pod comes out in one piece.

## Filters, sediment and cholesterol

The American Press and a French press both filter through metal, and an AeroPress normally uses paper. That matters for more than grit. Paper holds back cafestol and kahweol, the compounds in coffee oils behind unfiltered coffee's effect on LDL cholesterol, according to a review by Rob Urgert and Martijn Katan; boiled and plunger coffee, which skip paper, raise it. We found no measurement for the American Press itself. If you drink several cups a day through a metal filter and are watching your cholesterol, ask your clinician whether paper is worth it. Our [French press guide](/blog/how-to-use-a-french-press-the-most-amazing-coffee-youve-ever-had/) covers what a 2025 study measured in one French press brew.

## Our verdict (2020)

Yes, with a condition. In our video we liked it as an all-in-one brewer: compact, good for travel, and with a plastic outer wall, so it's probably harder to break than a glass press. Brewing takes not much longer than a French press, and the easy cleanup is its biggest selling point. But if you want the most flavor from your beans, it isn't the brewer we'd choose; the AeroPress beat it in our tasting. If you want convenience, it's a good buy.
