We bought a SteepShot soon after it went on sale in 2020 and reviewed it on our channel. The tasting, the problems and the verdict below are from that video. What's changed since, and the specifications, come from the sellers' own pages, with the dates we checked them.

## What the SteepShot is

The SteepShot was designed by Ari Halonen in Norway. A Kickstarter campaign run from Oslo raised NOK 388,258 from 528 backers, according to the creators' own update of April 18, 2018, and the Oslo roaster Tim Wendelboe distributed it; SteepShot's own site still quotes him on it. By 2020 it was on sale; in our video we bought it from Tim Wendelboe's website.

It's a single-cup brewer with a sealed chamber. You add coffee and hot water, screw on the lid, and wait. SteepShot's own site says pressure builds once the lid is on, which helps the water into the coffee and shortens the brew, and that releasing it pulls the water out of the grounds fast; it calls the method patented. Hoshikawa Cafe, which now sells it, describes it as immersion combined with steam pressure. To serve, you flip the SteepShot over your cup and open the deflector on the lid, and the coffee shoots out through a filter disc.

In 2020, Workshop Coffee, a roaster that sold it, described the brewing chamber as double-walled stainless steel, so you can hold it full of hot water. It came with a metal filter disc with 100-micron holes, a 60-micron disc was available, and AeroPress paper filters fit too.

## Our first brew: following the instructions

We followed its instructions and the demonstration on the SteepShot website:

- **Coffee:** 14 g of a medium-roast Costa Rican SHB, ground medium-fine.
- **Water:** 200 g, boiling, poured in fast. The instructions say speed matters, so a gooseneck kettle pours too slowly; use an ordinary kettle.
- **Steep:** screw the lid on tight, give it a swirl, wait 30 seconds.
- **Release:** flip it over the cup and open the deflector.

It lives up to its first claim: the coffee comes out fast, and the release is satisfying. But the cup was cloudy, perhaps from the mesh filter we used, and lighter in color than these beans usually brew. "Burnt and under-extracted" was the only way we could describe the taste. Someone who doesn't drink much coffee might settle for it; if you care about coffee, you won't.

**Our box had no filter.** The SteepShot website said it should come with metal filters, which are the same size as AeroPress filters. We happened to own some, so we used an AeroPress mesh disc. A first-time buyer without one couldn't have brewed at all. We contacted SteepShot about it.

## Our second brew: adjusting

For the second cup we changed four things at once: an AeroPress paper filter instead of the mesh, a medium-coarse grind, cooler water at about 203°F&nbsp;(95°C), and a 40-second steep instead of 30. We had to use the gooseneck to get that temperature, since we didn't own a temperature-control kettle.

There was much less pressure this time, which is probably why the instructions want boiling water poured fast. The paper also slowed the coffee down as it came out. The cup was much clearer, though still a little light in color, and it tasted okay. Better, but our takeaway was that the SteepShot needs real experimenting to find a recipe that works for you, which sits awkwardly with a brewer sold on simplicity.

| Recipe | Coffee and water | Steep before release |
|---|---|---|
| Our first try (2020) | 14 g, 200 g boiling (about 1:14) | 30 s, then flip and release |
| Our second try (2020) | 14 g, 200 g at 203°F&nbsp;(95°C) | 40 s |
| Workshop Coffee (2020) | 16 g, 250 g boiling (about 1:16) | Swirl, flip at 0:50, release at 1:00 |
| Hoshikawa Cafe, original model | 14 g, 200 g just boiled (about 1:14) | 55–60 s, then 10 s upside down |
| Hoshikawa Cafe, V2 | 15 g, 230 g at 203°F&nbsp;(95°C) or hotter (about 1:15) | 40 s, then 50 s upside down |

The ratios are calculated from each recipe's amounts. The three later recipes all steep longer than the 30 seconds of our first try, which fits what we found: 30 seconds just didn't feel like enough. Hoshikawa Cafe also has you preheat the SteepShot with hot water first, and with the V2 adds the water before the coffee.

## The pros

- **Simple, once it's together.** Working out the top section takes a moment; after that it's coffee, water, wait, release.
- **Easy to clean,** but not easier than an AeroPress or a dripper. In our view there isn't much in it between a French press, an American Press, a SteepShot, an AeroPress or a V60: each needs a different bit of attention, and none takes much longer than another.

## The cons

- **The coffee was only okay.** If you want the best coffee you can make at home, this isn't the way. It makes good enough coffee if convenience matters more to you.
- **No filter in our box,** as above.
- **The price.** The SteepShot itself was about €60 (about £55) on Tim Wendelboe's site, but we also paid €30 for shipping and a £20 customs bill, so it cost us over £100 to get one. What you'd pay depended on where you lived.
- **Early build quality.** The top section of ours had a lot of marks from machining; it almost looked 3D-printed. Hoshikawa Cafe, which imported the original into Japan, later wrote that nearly every unit from that run had small chips on the screw top from packing, and that it polished them by hand before shipping.

## Our verdict (2020)

A brilliant idea that isn't quite there yet. SteepShot is onto something, and if the coffee had been amazing, fast and easy, we'd have been happy at £100. But we were a little unimpressed, and for our money we'd rather have bought something else than the SteepShot as it was then. We hoped a new version would fix the problems we had, and ship with a filter.

## What happened next

Hoshikawa Cafe says it ended sales of the original SteepShot, which it had sold until June 2023, and now offers the redesigned V2. The Japanese coffee shop keeps its page for the original up for comparison with the V2. When checked October 18, 2026, SteepShot's own site sent buyers to a links page whose only listed shop was Hoshikawa Cafe, and Tim Wendelboe's old SteepShot product page returned "not found." We didn't find the SteepShot in a search on Amazon.

The SteepShot V2 was redesigned in Japan. As published by Hoshikawa Cafe, checked October 18, 2026:

- **Price:** listed at ¥9,890 to ¥10,880 including tax, in three colors.
- **Size and capacity:** 213 × 118 mm; 280 cc.
- **Materials:** polypropylene, silicone, stainless steel and ceramic; heat-resistant to 212°F&nbsp;(100°C); dishwasher safe.
- **Changes:** a ceramic coating inside, meant to cut metallic smells; a deflector arm for steadier flipping; a narrower outlet to reduce spurting; a stronger screw thread; and a built-in gasket that's harder to lose.
- **Filters:** a stainless steel filter comes with it, for a slightly cloudy cup; AeroPress paper filters give a clearer one.

Our review covers only the original.

## If you want fast coffee from a small brewer

The SteepShot takes AeroPress-size filters, and the presenter of our video was an AeroPress user, so it's the obvious comparison. AeroPress gives its own method a prep and clean-up time of less than 2 minutes. Our [AeroPress guide](/blog/why-and-how-you-should-use-an-aeropress/) has its maker's method, and our [Fellow Prismo review](/blog/aeropress-accessories-prismo-blind-taste-test-an-honest-review/) covers a pressure valve for the AeroPress.
