Our 2020 video calls this the C&C Ice Cream Float: coffee, cola and ice cream. It uses a scoop of soy ice cream, and its description says to choose your favorite, soy or dairy. The video gives a scoop, a shot and "top up" rather than measured amounts, so the cola amount below is our estimate for a 12 fl oz glass. The video predates this revision.

## The float

**For one float, in a 12 fl oz (355 ml) glass:**

- **Ice cream:** 1 scoop, dairy or soy.
- **Espresso:** 1 single shot, about 1 fl oz (25–30 ml).
- **Cola:** about 6 fl oz (180 ml), chilled, to top up. The video's description lists Coca-Cola.
- **Time:** about 3 minutes, plus pulling the shot.

**Method:**

1. Scoop the ice cream into the bottom of a tall glass.
2. Pour the shot of espresso over the ice cream.
3. Top up with cola, pouring it slowly down the side of the glass to keep the foam down.
4. Stir gently and serve with a straw.

The video's description warns that cola and espresso "can result in a lot of fizz," and says to keep every component, and the glasses, very cold.

The float starts as an affogato, espresso poured over ice cream, before the cola goes in. Our [affogato guide](/blog/affogato-recipe-guide/) covers that half on its own.

## Hot or cold coffee?

Here the shot goes in freshly brewed, as the video's description has it, but it lands on the ice cream first. For drinks where the soda goes straight onto the coffee, our advice is to use chilled coffee instead. Carbon dioxide dissolves less in warmer water. Stir a 1 fl oz shot at about 158°F&nbsp;(70°C) into 6 fl oz of soda from the fridge at 39°F&nbsp;(4°C), and the mix lands near 56°F&nbsp;(13°C), counting both as water and leaving out the glass and the ice. By the Henry's-law constants in NIST's Chemistry WebBook, water at that temperature holds about a quarter less carbon dioxide than it does at 39°F (both figures calculated). A hot shot costs fizz; cold coffee keeps more of it.

If you'd rather not use a hot shot at all, pour 2 to 3 fl oz of chilled cold brew over the ice cream instead. It melts the ice cream more slowly, and the float tastes of cold brew rather than espresso.

## How much caffeine?

Two things in the glass carry caffeine. The Caffeine Calculator puts a single espresso shot at 60–75 mg. Coca-Cola lists 34 mg in a 12 fl oz can, so about 6 fl oz of it adds about 17 mg (calculated). That makes about 77–92 mg for the float. A bigger glass takes more cola, and each extra fl oz of Coca-Cola adds about 3 mg (calculated). The same 6 fl oz of cola also adds about 20 g of sugar, from Coca-Cola's 39 g per can (calculated); the ice cream's label gives its own.

For less caffeine, Coca-Cola also makes Coca-Cola Caffeine Free. Its Mexico version is made with cane sugar instead of high fructose corn syrup, and Coca-Cola says it contains caffeine too. Decaf espresso still has a little caffeine; our [decaf guide](/blog/how-is-coffee-decaffeinated/) explains how much.

## Swaps

- **Root beer** in place of the cola makes a root beer float; our [coffee and root beer](/blog/mixing-coffee-and-root-beer-delicious/) page has a cold brew drink built on it.
- **Cream soda** in place of the cola gives a vanilla float; for a drink without ice cream, see our [coffee and cream soda](/blog/mixing-coffee-andcream-soda/).
- **Coffee ice cream** makes it taste more of coffee.
