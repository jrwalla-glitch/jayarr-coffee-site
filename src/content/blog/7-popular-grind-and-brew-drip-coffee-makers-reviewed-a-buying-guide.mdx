A grind-and-brew coffee maker grinds whole beans straight into its filter basket and then brews, so a pot you program the night before starts from freshly ground coffee. In January 2021 we looked at seven popular models and ranked them from worst to best in the video below. This page keeps those verdicts, adds what each maker publishes about its machine today, checked October 18, 2026, and says plainly which ones you can still buy.

## Why grind just before brewing

Jessica Easto explains in *Craft Coffee: A Manual* that the flavor and aroma compounds locked in a bean start to fade once it's ground: a trained palate can notice the loss about 30 minutes after grinding, and most people after an hour or more. Her more practical reason is size. Pre-ground coffee is unlikely to be ground right for your brewer, and buying it takes away the one adjustment you'd make when a cup goes wrong. A grind-and-brew fixes the first problem automatically, and the better ones give you some of the second.

The case has limits. In a few informal blind tests, Scott Rao reports, every taster preferred coffee ground 12 hours ahead to coffee ground just before brewing, though in his drip trials the pre-ground samples were also ground slightly finer. He concluded that pre-grinding isn't harmful to flavor and sometimes helps. Our [batch brew guide](/blog/batch-brew-drip-coffee-guide/) has the details, along with stirring, batch size and holding.

## What to look for

**A burr grinder.** James Hoffmann explains in *The World Atlas of Coffee* that a blade grinder smashes coffee into some very fine powder and some very large pieces; in the brew, the powder quickly adds bitterness and the large pieces add sourness. Easto adds that the fines clog paper filters, slowing the water and over-extracting the cup. A burr grinder cuts the beans between two burrs set a chosen distance apart, so the grounds come out more even. No grinder makes a single size, but in Jonathan Gagné's measurements lower-quality grinders make wider spreads of sizes than better ones. Breville, Capresso, Cuisinart and Krups all describe burr grinders in their own manuals or pages.

**How it decides the dose.** Most of these machines grind by cup count and a strength button rather than by weight, and their "cups" differ. Cuisinart's 12 cups come to 54 oz, or 4.5 oz each, while Black+Decker counts about 5 oz a cup and Breville's 12-cup tank holds 60 oz (calculated, 5 oz a cup). Breville is the one maker here with a calibration mode: you weigh what the grinder delivers in 10 seconds on a scale that reads to 1 gram and enter it, and the machine corrects its grinding times for your beans. Whatever you own, weighing one batch of its grounds tells you what strength you're really brewing.

**A grinder you can switch off.** Breville, Capresso, Cuisinart, Krups and Black+Decker all let you turn the grinder off and brew pre-ground coffee, which is handy for a decaf or a bag someone gives you.

**A grinder you can clean.** Ground coffee builds up in the chute between the burrs and the basket, and each maker sets its own routine:

- **Breville** shows "CLEAN COFFEE CHUTE" when the burrs and chute need brushing, which it says may be every two to three weeks, and the upper burr lifts out.
- **Krups** says to brush out the grinding compartment at least every two weeks so it doesn't clog.
- **Cuisinart** warns that a chute left uncleaned builds up grounds that can make the coffee taste weak.
- **Capresso** says to watch the feeder channel's window for oil and buildup. It also warns that sugar- or powder-coated beans, such as cinnamon or vanilla flavors, can stick to the burrs and, over time, reduce the grinder's performance, and that putting coated beans in the bean container voids its warranty; for flavored coffee, it suggests adding a few drops of liquid flavoring to the carafe instead.
- **Black+Decker** has you wash its combined grinder and filter basket by hand after every use.

For descaling, follow your machine's manual: Breville suggests monthly in soft water and more often in hard water, Black+Decker a vinegar cycle once a month, Krups a cycle every 70 brews, when its indicator flashes, and Capresso at least twice a year.

**A thermal carafe, not a hot plate.** Brewed coffee doesn't hold well, and a hot plate makes it worse. Rao, writing for cafés, says flavor loss is noticeable within 15 to 20 minutes whatever the holding conditions and keeps coffee in a sealed thermal container. Easto, writing for home brewers, finds that a good preheated thermos or insulated carafe can keep coffee surprisingly fresh for several hours. Three of the seven machines here brew into a thermal carafe.

## Still sold: our verdicts and what the makers say now

The verdicts below are from our 2021 video. The "Now" notes give what each maker publishes, checked October 18, 2026.

### Breville Grind Control (BDC650BSS): our top pick

**Our 2021 verdict:** many call it the best grind-and-brew coffee maker, and we tended to agree. Its stainless-steel burrs adjust in steps small enough to suit different roasts or origins, and for a drip machine, the video said, those are the adjustments that matter rather than the extremes of coarse and fine. Its eight strength settings change how much coffee it grinds for the same water; it brews 2 to 12 cups into a thermal stainless-steel carafe, or a single cup; and it can start itself at a set time. It earned our top spot for its grinding, its programmability and its overall completeness. It was listed at about $300 in our video.

**Now:** Breville listed the Grind Control at $399.95 on its US store, in stock, when checked. Its manual describes flat stainless-steel burrs with six grind settings, and says setting 3 should cover most coffees: 2 to 3 for a 10 oz cup, 3 to 5 for a full carafe. It has eight strength settings plus a pre-ground setting that turns the grinder off, a half-pound bean hopper, a 60 oz water tank, a 12-cup double-wall stainless thermal carafe and a gold-tone filter (or an 8–12 cup flat-bottom paper filter). The single-cup mode has seven sizes and takes a cup or travel mug up to 7.5 in tall and 2¾ in across once the drip tray is removed. Breville covers it with a 1-year limited warranty and says not to put any of its parts in the dishwasher. To buy: [Breville's store](https://www.breville.com/en-us/product/bdc650), or the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Breville+BDC650BSS+Grind+Control&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Breville Grind Control BDC650BSS</a> on Amazon.

### Krups Grind and Brew Auto-Start (KM785D50): fourth in our ranking

**Our 2021 verdict:** a minimalist design with a clear LCD and intuitive controls that let you set most things, including brew strength. Its burr grinder was the real selling point: the video called it a dramatic step up in consistency from the Melitta's and Black+Decker's grinders, and few grind-and-brews around $150 had one; its five settings cover any reasonable drip grind. It has pause and serve. The downsides were a glass carafe on a hot plate and reliability complaints from some online reviewers. It was an option to consider if you're on a budget and still want a proper grind, listed at $130 to $170 in our video.

**Now:** Krups still lists the KM785D50 on its US site, without a price of its own; it sends buyers to retailers. Krups describes a 10-cup machine with a five-setting conical burr grinder, programmable auto-start, a two-hour keep-warm, a bold setting, a glass carafe, a gold-tone permanent filter and pause and serve. Its manual says to brush out the grinding compartment at least every two weeks and to descale after 70 brewing cycles. Amazon's results for it show other machines and accessories, so the place to start is [Krups's page](https://www.krupsusa.com/BREAKFAST-APPLIANCES/Filter-Coffee-Makers/FCM-GRIND-AND-BREW-KM785/p/1500637792).

### Black+Decker Mill & Brew (CM5000B): fifth in our ranking

**Our 2021 verdict:** its 24-hour programming suits a hectic schedule, it switches the grinder off for pre-ground coffee, and it adjusts the strength. A window shows the water level, the glass carafe holds 12 cups, Sneak-A-Cup lets you pour a cup before the brew finishes, and every part detaches for cleaning. The drawback, as with the Melitta, was the grinder, which the video called a blade grinder with a pretty inconsistent grind. Overall it was inexpensive and feature-rich, a reasonable option on a budget, listed at about $60 in our video.

**A correction:** the video and this page used to say you could adjust its grind size. Black+Decker's manual says its Grind Select button "cycles through optimal grind settings (4, 6, 8, 10, 12) correlating with the number of cups to be brewed," so the machine sets the grind for the size of the pot, not for your taste.

**Now:** Black+Decker listed the CM5000B at $89.99 on its store, in stock, when checked. Its 2023 manual describes a 12-cup machine (about 5 oz a cup) with a "2-in-1 grinder and permanent filter basket," in which the beans are ground and brewed in the same assembly; it doesn't name the type of grinder. It has three strengths (regular, strong and bold), a hot plate that switches off after up to 2 hours, and a 2-year limited warranty. Black+Decker says to wash the grinder basket and showerhead by hand after every use and to run a vinegar cleaning cycle once a month. To buy: [Black+Decker's store](https://blackanddeckerappliances.com/products/buy-the-12-cup-mill-brew-coffeemaker-cm5000b), or the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Black+Decker+CM5000B+Coffee+Maker&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Black+Decker CM5000B Mill & Brew</a> on Amazon.

## No longer sold by their makers

### Capresso CoffeeTEAM TS (465): second in our ranking

**Our 2021 verdict:** many automatic drip machines don't make great coffee next to manual brewing, and the Capresso still won't match a pour-over or an AeroPress, but its design aims at a better brew. It has a burr grinder with five settings from fine to coarse and can be used with or without it, a 6 oz bean container that holds enough for several brews, a charcoal water filter and a programmable timer. It brews 4, 6, 8 or 10 cups at mild, regular or strong, has pause and serve, and brews into a stainless thermal carafe that holds the heat for hours instead of scorching the coffee on a hot plate. The one drawback: grinding takes a while and is somewhat loud. It checked almost every important box, listed at about $200 in our video.

**Now:** Capresso's US store doesn't list the CoffeeTEAM TS, and it names no replacement. The grind-and-brew it does list is the CoffeeTEAM GS (464.05), with a 10-cup glass carafe on a warming plate, listed at $219.99 when checked. Capresso's manual for the TS, hosted on JURA's store, describes conical burrs with five settings calibrated for drip coffee: coarser for darker, oilier beans and finer for light or medium roasts. It says the thermal carafe keeps coffee hot for up to 4 hours.

### Cuisinart Burr Grind & Brew Thermal (DGB-900BC): third

**Our 2021 verdict:** it checks pretty much every box, but at a hefty price. Stainless-steel construction; 24-hour programming with automatic brewing and shut-off; a grind-off button for pre-ground coffee; a burr grinder for an even grind; a hopper for 8 oz of beans; a double-wall thermal carafe for 12 cups with no hot plate; and a charcoal water filter. At its typical price, listed at about $400 in our video, it wasn't one we could easily recommend, given cheaper machines on this list with similar or better performance.

**A correction:** the video said you could fine-tune its grind size. Cuisinart's manual says the machine is "preset for a medium grind," and its Grind Control knob chooses how much coffee to grind, not how fine.

**Now:** Cuisinart's page for the DGB-900BC is headed "Discontinued" and shows it out of stock, and names no replacement. Cuisinart still sells its parts, such as the gold-tone filter and charcoal water filters. Its manual gives 12 cups as 54 oz, a half-pound bean hopper, three strengths and a limited three-year warranty.

### Melitta Mill & Brew (MEMB1B): sixth

**Our 2021 verdict:** a no-frills design without a learning curve. Its clock can set the grind and brew one day ahead, the glass carafe holds 10 cups, and the hot plate switches off after 60 minutes. The grinder has seven settings, though most aren't very useful; a medium grind is best for drip. The video called it a blade grinder with a pretty inconsistent grind. The stainless-steel permanent filter basket is easy to clean, and a pause-and-serve feature lets you pour before the brew finishes. A low-budget option, but somewhat hard to recommend, listed at $70 to $80 in our video.

**Now:** Melitta's US store sells its filters, pour-over cones and coffee, but doesn't list the Mill & Brew.

### Gourmia GCM4500: seventh

**Our 2021 verdict:** built for simplicity. The five-button LCD is very easy to use but limited: you can program a brew ahead, but not change its strength. It looks modern in black and stainless steel and fits a small counter at 11 × 7 × 12 in. The hopper holds about 2 oz of beans, enough for one pot. The video called it a blade grinder that doesn't produce a quality grind, and the glass carafe sits on a hot plate that can burn coffee left too long. Listed at about $100 in our video, its deficiencies were impossible to overlook.

**Now:** Gourmia's product catalog doesn't list the GCM4500; it lists other grind-and-brew models.

## Grind-and-brews on the SCA's certified list

The Specialty Coffee Association certifies home brewers that pass third-party testing at its testing center against its home-brewer standards, for a three-year licensing period; the current standards are in its members' portal, and our [batch brew guide](/blog/batch-brew-drip-coffee-guide/) quotes the temperature rules from the 2017 program. When we checked the SCA's list on October 18, 2026, it included two grind-and-brew machines, both certified in 2023 and both made by GE Appliances. Neither was in our 2021 video; the specs are as published by the maker.

- **Café Specialty Grind and Brew** (C7CGAAS2TS3, stainless steel), listed at $329.00 by Café: a conical burr grinder with six settings and a replaceable burr, a 200 g hopper, a 10-cup (45 oz) thermal carafe, single cups of 6 to 24 oz into a mug up to 7 in tall, four strengths, a brewing temperature range of 185 to 205°F&nbsp;(85 to 96°C), Wi-Fi app control and a 1-year limited warranty. [Café's page](https://www.cafeappliances.com/appliance/CAFE-Specialty-Grind-and-Brew-Coffee-Maker-with-Thermal-Carafe-C7CGAAS2TS3).
- **GE Profile Smart Grind and Brew** (P7CGAAS2TSS, stainless steel), listed at $399.00 by GE Appliances: a conical burr grinder with six settings and a replaceable burr, a 190 g hopper, a 10-cup (45 oz) thermal carafe, a 90 oz removable tank, five strengths, adjustable bloom time, the same 185 to 205°F&nbsp;(85 to 96°C) range, Wi-Fi and a 1-year limited warranty. [GE Appliances' page](https://www.geappliances.com/appliance/GE-Profile-Smart-Grind-and-Brew-Coffee-Maker-with-Podless-Single-Serve-P7CGAAS2TSS).

The list changes as certifications are granted and expire, so check the [SCA's certified home equipment list](https://sca.coffee/certified-home-brewer) before you buy.

## Or pair a burr grinder with a drip machine

A grind-and-brew saves counter space and a step in the morning, but its grinder is built into a machine you'll replace as a whole. Easto's advice is that if you'll invest in only one piece of coffee equipment, it should be a decent grinder, and a separate one lets you set the grind and weigh the dose yourself. Our [electric grinder guide](/blog/top-5-best-electric-coffee-grinders/) compares five, and our [batch brew guide](/blog/batch-brew-drip-coffee-guide/) covers brewing a drip pot by weight.
