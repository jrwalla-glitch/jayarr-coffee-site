What if you could skip brewing entirely and spread your morning caffeine on toast? In June 2020 we tried **Nudge Coffee Butter** on video, in two flavors. This page keeps that review as the video has it. The product facts were checked on October 18, 2026.

*Correction, October 2026: earlier versions of this page said two tablespoons held about the caffeine of an 8-ounce cup of coffee, called the Italian roast richer and more sophisticated than the Colombian, and gave the Colombian 20 beans a serving. In the video, the Italian tasted less bold than the Colombian, and the jars were read as 40 beans a serving for the Colombian and 26 for the Italian. The maker's own 40 mg is well short of a cup. The page now follows the video and the maker's figures.*

## What it was

As published by its maker, Tierra Nueva of Miami, in April 2019: Nudge Coffee Butter was made with the company's "whole bean coffee technology," and "a serving of Nudge Coffee Butter has 40mg of caffeine, uses 20 whole coffee beans and is free of nuts and chocolate." It was produced with Nutkao USA, a maker of nut and cocoa spreads, and was due to launch in June 2019.

That April, at the Specialty Coffee Expo in Boston, the Specialty Coffee Association's Best New Product awards named Tierra Nueva's Nudge Coffee Butter the winner in the food category. The company said the winning flavor was Brazilian Origin.

The two jars in our video were the **Colombian Original** and the **Italian Espresso Roast**. Each label rated its strength on a five-point scale: 3, the middle, for the Colombian, and 4, closer to the darker end, for the Italian. Going by the jars, the video said two tablespoons gave you the equivalent of one cup of coffee, and that the Colombian used 40 beans a serving and the darker Italian 26.

## The taste test

**Colombian Original.** We spread a little on toast to get a true sense of it at breakfast, then went back for a good second tablespoon. It had a beautiful rich flavor and was smooth and creamy. If they didn't say there was no chocolate in it, you'd swear there was: it really tasted like a chocolate mocha coffee. We couldn't help licking the knife.

**Italian Espresso Roast.** Pretty good, and it didn't taste too dark. It tasted fairly similar to the Colombian but wasn't quite as bold. Still delicious.

**The verdict.** The Colombian was more to our liking: bold, creamy, and like a chocolate mocha. What we loved was the convenience. There was no waiting 10 minutes to brew a cup and add cream and sugar; make toast, spread it on, and you're out the door with breakfast and your caffeine in one. You could put it on waffles or a bagel too, or dip strawberries, raspberries or other fruit in it. It was so good we could have sat there and eaten the whole jar: maybe one of our favorite ways to eat coffee, and we recommended it.

## How its caffeine compares with a cup

The "one cup of coffee" equivalence doesn't hold up against the maker's own number. At 40 mg a serving, two tablespoons held about a fifth to two-fifths of what our Caffeine Calculator estimates for an 8 fl oz (237 ml) drip coffee, 105–195 mg, and a little over half to two-thirds of a single espresso's 60–75 mg (calculated). The maker gave one figure for "a serving"; we found no figures for each flavor.

The video counted the spread as part of breakfast. We make no health claims for it: the maker's claims were about caffeine, beans and ingredients it left out, and the agencies' guidance on caffeine is in our [caffeine in coffee guide](/blog/caffeine-in-coffee-guide/).

## Can you still buy it?

It appears to be no longer sold. The trade press gave Nudge's own website as the place to buy it online, but when we checked that address on October 18, 2026, it showed an unrelated site. An Amazon search for "Nudge Coffee Butter" the same day returned other products. The brand was still around in January 2021, when the trade magazine *Confectionery News* reported Nudge bars, "bombs" and butters in 350 Sprouts stores, under a brand owner named as The Whole Coffee Co.

If it's the mocha taste you're after, our guide to [making a mocha](/blog/making-a-mocha-with-and-without-espresso-machine/) covers the drink. How roasting creates coffee's flavor in the first place is in our guide to [how coffee is roasted](/blog/how-is-coffee-roasted/).
