## Paper or metal: what changes

AeroPress, which sells both kinds, explains the difference in its own filter guide. Paper absorbs oils and holds back fine particles, for a cup with clear, discernible flavors, a light body and no grit; thicker paper holds back more. Metal lets oils and fine particles into the cup, which gives a heavier texture but tends to mask delicate flavors, and larger holes let more and bigger particles through.

The case for metal in our video was mostly about waste. A paper filter is single-use for most people; we found we got about five brews out of one before we noticed a different taste, but that's still paper. A metal filter never runs out.

There's also a health difference. Paper holds back cafestol and kahweol, the compounds in coffee oils behind unfiltered coffee's effect on LDL cholesterol, according to a review by Rob Urgert and Martijn Katan; boiled and plunger coffee, which skip paper, raise it. A metal AeroPress filter lets those oils through, though we found no measurement of how much ends up in an AeroPress cup. If you drink several cups a day through metal and are watching your cholesterol, ask your clinician whether paper is worth sticking with. Our [French press guide](/blog/how-to-use-a-french-press-the-most-amazing-coffee-youve-ever-had/) covers what a 2025 study measured in one French press brew.

## The three filters (2020)

We picked three of the most common metal filters sold online at the time and hadn't brewed with any of them before the video:

- **Cafe Concetto fine filter disc,** the most expensive on Amazon, about £18 ($22) as listed in 2020. A thick, copper-colored disc that doesn't bend easily, with fewer, larger holes than the Able. It came in raw cardboard packaging with a brew-tip card, but the disc itself was wrapped in plastic.
- **Able Disk Fine,** about £16 ($19) as listed in 2020. A thinner, silver disc that bends more easily, with more, smaller holes than the Cafe Concetto. Its box said 100% made in the USA, with a recipe card, and no plastic at all.
- **Koffico mesh filter,** the cheapest at £6.99 (about $8) as listed in 2020. Not a punched disc but a fine metal gauze. It came in a printed cardboard box with no instructions, wrapped in plastic.

An earlier version of this page called the third filter a CoffeeSock mesh filter. The on-screen caption in our video names it the Koffico filter, and the page now does too.

## The recipe we used

The two brew-tip cards gave slightly different recipes, so for a fair comparison we brewed all four cups the same way. It's a variation on Wendelien van Bunnik's 2019 World AeroPress Championship recipe that we'd been brewing daily for a month, adapted for one cup and with a short bloom added:

1. 16 g of coarsely ground coffee, with the AeroPress inverted.
2. Pour about 60 g of water and fold the grounds in with the stirrer, just to wet them all. Wait about 30 seconds.
3. Add about 40 g more, to 100 g, and stir vigorously 20 times.
4. Put on the cap with the filter (for paper, a pre-wetted one) and push out the air.
5. Wait about 40 seconds, then flip it onto the cup and press slowly.
6. Dilute with 120 g of water.

That's 16 g of coffee to 220 g of water in all, about 1:14, brewed at about 1:6 in the chamber before diluting (calculated). Van Bunnik's own winning recipe, on the World AeroPress Championship's page, uses 30 g of coffee and 100 g of water at 198°F&nbsp;(92°C), flips and presses at 0:40, then dilutes with 100 g of water or more, to taste.

**A safety note.** Like van Bunnik's, our recipe is brewed inverted. AeroPress now strongly discourages the inverted method, because the brewer is less stable upside down and can tip over and spill hot coffee. To brew upright, use AeroPress's own method in our [AeroPress guide](/blog/why-and-how-you-should-use-an-aeropress/), or its Flow Control Filter Cap, which stops drips and takes AeroPress's paper and metal filters.

## What we found

| Filter | In the cup (2020) |
|---|---|
| Paper (control) | Tasted as we expected from this coffee |
| Able Disk Fine | Fine sediment, and some grounds on the bottom despite the coarse grind; more of the fruity notes came through |
| Cafe Concetto Fine | A little sediment, mostly risen to the top, and less than the Able; tasted as expected |
| Koffico mesh | No sediment at all, almost as clean as the paper cup; it tasted fine |

None of the metal filters seemed to change the taste of the coffee much. What disappointed us was that the Able and the Cafe Concetto both let sediment through to the bottom of the cup, which we don't expect from an AeroPress. The Able let through far more than the Cafe Concetto, which surprised us, because its holes are smaller.

We found it hard to pick a favorite. If we had to choose one, it was the Koffico mesh: it was the cheapest, and it let no grounds through. But any of the three is fine, and metal filters make AeroPress coffee that tastes just as good as paper. With the Cafe Concetto or the Able, you may need a coarser grind than usual, because of the grounds they let through.

## What each maker sells now

**As published by each maker,** checked October 18, 2026:

- **Able Disk.** Able Brewing lists the Disk Fine at $15. It has 152-micron holes, is made of 304 stainless steel in the USA, and is a third as thick as Able's Standard disk, so Able says to handle it with care because it bends and damages more easily. The Standard, with 254-micron holes, is thicker and sold in a set with the Fine; there's also a Fine XL for the AeroPress XL. Able describes the Fine as giving a very sweet, clean cup with "very little fines to none at all," which isn't what we found in our video. On Amazon, a search for the <a href="https://www.amazon.com/s?k=Able+Brewing+Disk+Fine+Filter+AeroPress&tag=jayarrcoffee-20" rel="sponsored noopener">Able Disk Fine</a> returns Able's own listing first, sold by Able Brewing at $15 when checked October 18, 2026.
- **Cafe Concetto.** Its site lists two discs, Fine and Superfine, plus XL sizes for the AeroPress XL. It says the Fine keeps more of the coffee's natural oils for more body, and the Superfine gives a cleaner cup with minimal sediment. It lists them as fitting the AeroPress Original, Go and Premium, and gives no hole size or price. The Amazon search we checked showed the Superfine, not the Fine we used, so we link the [maker's page](https://www.cafeconcetto.com/filter-discs-for-use-with-aeropress) instead.
- **Koffico.** We found no maker's page for it: koffico.com now redirects to a page offering the domain for sale, and an Amazon search for it returned other brands. We couldn't confirm it's still made.
- **AeroPress's own.** AeroPress listed its Stainless Steel Filter at $14.95, with holes laser-cut to 178 microns, made in the USA from 316 stainless steel, for the Original, Clear and Go but not the XL, and a titanium-coated Gold Tone filter with smaller holes at $19.95. AeroPress argues that its laser-cut discs last longer than woven mesh filters, which it says fray over time and may not seal as well; that's a maker comparing its own product.

## When to stick with paper

Paper gives the clearest cup, as AeroPress's own guide says, and it's what holds back the cafestol. If you want the most delicate flavors from a coffee, brew it through paper. Our suggestion is to keep both: paper for coffees you want to taste clearly, metal for everyday cups and for travel.

Our video also said that no World AeroPress Championship barista had ever used anything but paper, and an earlier version of this page repeated it. That wasn't right even then. The championship's own recipe page lists podium recipes that put a metal filter in the cap along with a paper one: Jeongsu Park's, third in 2017, and Benja Khemacheva's, second in 2019. Most of the recipes listed there use paper only.

If you'd like a valve that stops drips instead, our [Fellow Prismo review](/blog/aeropress-accessories-prismo-blind-taste-test-an-honest-review/) tasted one blind against a standard and an inverted AeroPress.
